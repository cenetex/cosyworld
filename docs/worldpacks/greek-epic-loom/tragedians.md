# Tragic houses: 14 strands for the Wine-Dark poem

[Read the full poem plan and chapter map](README.md).

Design research for Aeschylus and Sophocles. All verse below is original. All playable tasks and alternate results below are proposed CosyWorld adaptations. Each source paragraph states what the play supplies. A source-aligned result follows a source event or moral conflict; the player's role remains an invention.

The live opening remains the Theban aftermath. Earlier events enter as named recalled scenes. The player enters a song through a speaker, relic, or shrine; the return brings an account, promise, or custom into the present. Choices in a recalled scene shape that telling and its present effect. A chosen alternate history needs an explicit account label. Each card carries one short offer couplet. Its action label states the task in plain words. Completing a beat adds a lasting stanza. The final result couplet below closes each play's strand.

The proposed memory keys name future saved facts. Existing `greek-atreid-account` and `greek-theban-response` govern the current choices. Carry specific deeds and witnesses between chapters through named events.

## 1. Persians — Aeschylus

- Work: `aeschylus-persians`. Place: `greek-susa` (The Court at Susa).
- Time: a separately named court account. Its historical war sits outside the Theban family sequence. Travel first establishes which account the court is telling.
- Source: Atossa receives the report of defeat. Darius's ghost warns of the king's excess. Xerxes returns to shared mourning. The play gives grief to the defeated Persian court. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/persians.html).
- Poem question: Who enters a nation's song when its king comes home?

Three proposed quests:
1. **The broken oar.** Bring a survivor's broken oar to the court. Match his account to the messenger's named dead. Offer: “An oar has crossed the empty sea. / Whose hand once held its wood?”
2. **Water for the absent.** Help Atossa prepare the tomb offering. Let households add names before the royal prayer. Offer: “Pour water where the great king sleeps. / The small doors wait for news.”
3. **The torn cloak.** Meet Xerxes at the threshold. Choose the audience for his return: mourning households, the palace, or a public reckoning. Offer: “A king comes home in torn cloth. / Make room for all he carries.”

Branch endings:
- **Shared mourning:** the king names ordinary losses before the city. “We named the hands beneath the waves. / The king stood among the mourners.”
- **Private consolation:** the household tends Xerxes; families receive the full account later through the player. “We warmed the king beside the hearth. / Outside, the mothers kept their lamps.”
- **Public reckoning:** the court records the invasion's cost and appoints household messengers. This civic process is invented. “We set the broken oar upright. / The throne heard every empty house.”

Carry: `susa-dead-names`, `susa-return-audience`. The oar recurs in Trojan Women and Peace. A public naming creates a later option to read enemy names at a Greek feast. A private account creates a later duty to carry the withheld news.

## 2. Seven Against Thebes — Aeschylus

- Work: `aeschylus-seven-against-thebes`. Place: `greek-gate` (Theban City Gate).
- Time: recalled siege, named “Before the seventh gate.” Its closing returns to the existing aftermath.
- Source: a scout reports the seven attackers; Eteocles assigns defenders. He faces his brother at the seventh gate. Both brothers die while the city survives. Use the brothers' deaths as this frame's fixed horizon. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/seventhebes.html).
- Poem question: What does a saved city owe the households that survive it?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Seven shield signs.** Carry the scout's report to each gate captain. Ask which streets fall under each gate's shelter. Offer: “Seven shields shine beyond the wall. / Count the doors beneath their shadow.”
2. **The inner road.** Open a route for children, elders, and water carriers. Supplies spent here reduce the reserve for the gate. Offer: “The wall drinks iron through the night. / The houses ask for water.”
3. **Two names beyond the gate.** After the duel, lead the kin to a vigil outside the gate. Record the brothers' fate and the witness who saw it. Offer: “Two brothers lie beyond one gate. / Bring those who know their names.”

Branch endings:
- **Equal household grief:** bring the kin to mourn both brothers outside the gate. Polynices remains at the exposed battlefield for the opening burial story. “We spoke two names beyond the gate. / Their sisters shared the dust.”
- **State honors:** serve the public procession for Eteocles; preserve Polynices's location for Antigone. “One shield rose above the crowd. / We kept the other brother's place.”
- **Survivors first:** rescue trapped households before joining the mourning. “We carried children through the smoke. / At dawn, two mothers called the roll.”

Carry: `seventh-gate-witness`, `siege-shelter-route`, `brothers-mourning-account`. The witness gives the opening burial dispute a personal cost. The safe street later houses suppliants. The recalled scene adds a stanza about who was protected; the present burial decision stays with the existing story.

## 3. Suppliant Women — Aeschylus

- Work: `aeschylus-suppliant-women`. Place: `greek-argos-sanctuary` (Argive Shore Sanctuary).
- Time: a named Danaid account, heard at a living sanctuary. The present quest asks the current hosts to honor what their city claims.
- Source: the Danaids seek protection from forced marriage. Pelasgus weighs the city's danger; the people support protection. An Egyptian herald presses the claim to take the women away. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/suppliant.html).
- Poem question: When a city gives shelter, whose hands make that promise real?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Branches at the altar.** Bring water and bind the suppliant branches. Hear each woman's chosen terms of shelter. Offer: “Their branches tremble at the shore. / Bring water to the roots.”
2. **A roof for each name.** Ask households to offer beds, food, and watch shifts. Present these commitments to the assembly. Offer: “The city lifts a thousand hands. / Find roofs beneath the promise.”
3. **The herald's rope.** Escort the women through the shore gate when the herald arrives. Choose a protected home, guarded sanctuary, or freely chosen onward ship. Offer: “A rope lies ready by the tide. / Ask whose hands shall hold it.”

Branch endings:
- **Homes within the city:** the hosts accept both guests and a share of danger. “We opened houses toward the sea. / Their names grew warm beside our fires.”
- **Sanctuary under guard:** the shrine is protected while households debate longer settlement. “We kept the altar through the night. / The city learned the cost of watch.”
- **Chosen passage:** the women choose onward travel and receive escort and provisions. “They chose the sail; we filled the jars. / Their hands held the ropes.”

Carry: `argive-host-pledges`, `danaid-choice`, `shore-watch`. Links to Children of Heracles, Oedipus at Colonus, and Trojan Women. Passage must follow the women's consent; their safety is a continuing obligation in the poem.

<a id="aeschylus-prometheus-bound"></a>

## 4. Prometheus Bound — Aeschylus in the pack

- Work: `aeschylus-prometheus-bound`. Place: `greek-scythian-crag` (The Scythian Crag).
- Time: divine frontier, explicitly set apart from household time.
- Source: Prometheus suffers for helping mortals. Visitors hear his account; Io receives knowledge of her travels. Hermes demands a dangerous secret. The storm closes the play. The pack follows the traditional author label; modern authorship is debated. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/prometheus.html); [edition and attribution note](https://www.theoi.com/Text/AeschylusPrometheus.html).
- Poem question: What part of a gift can survive the power that punishes its giver?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Fire under stone.** Carry a covered ember from the crag to a cold shelter. Spend scarce fuel to keep it alive. Offer: “A coal keeps morning under stone. / Carry its small sun onward.”
2. **Io's road.** Mark safe water stops from Io's account. Let her choose who receives the route. Offer: “Her feet have crossed the world's sharp edge. / Set water on her road.”
3. **The storm witness.** When the demand for the secret arrives, choose to remain beside Prometheus, carry his teaching away, or send his stated terms through a messenger. Offer: “The sky has called its witness home. / Whose word will weather carry?”

Branch endings:
- **Stay through the storm:** risk safe passage to preserve a living witness. “We stood where thunder split the stone. / His voice had company.”
- **Carry the gifts:** pass fire and practical knowledge among households. “We took one coal into the rain. / A hundred hearths recalled his name.”
- **Carry his terms:** transmit his own words for a future bargain; any release remains a later event. “We bore his terms across the cloud. / The chain rang through the message.”

Carry: `prometheus-gift`, `io-road-consent`, `storm-witness`. Fire becomes the cooking fire in Peace and the beacon in Agamemnon. His chain echoes the human claim to shelter and consent.

<a id="aeschylus-agamemnon"></a>

## 5. Agamemnon — Aeschylus

- Work: `aeschylus-agamemnon`. Place: `greek-argos-palace` (Argive Palace). Account: `greek-atreid-account=aeschylus`.
- Time: recalled homecoming within the chosen Atreid account. The deaths of Agamemnon and Cassandra lead to the tomb strand; branches change testimony and survivor protection.
- Source: beacon fire announces Troy's fall. Agamemnon returns with Cassandra. Clytemnestra kills them; the remembered death of Iphigenia helps frame the household's grievance. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/agamemnon.html).
- Poem question: Whose voice survives the feast of victory?

Three proposed quests:
1. **The last beacon.** Tend the palace watch fire. Carry the herald's fuller account to the waiting houses. Offer: “The hills have passed a flame to us. / Ask what the brightness carries.”
2. **The captive's cup.** Bring water to Cassandra. Record her chosen words before the palace doors close. Offer: “A captive waits beside the feast. / Set down a cup and listen.”
3. **The opened doors.** Gather surviving servants after the killings. Choose where their account will first be heard. Offer: “The doors swing wide upon the house. / Gather the voices left inside.”

Branch endings:
- **Full household testimony:** carry Cassandra, Clytemnestra, and the servants' distinct accounts to the tomb. “We carried every voice outside. / The red cloth held their names.”
- **Queen's reckoning first:** give Iphigenia's story the first public hearing; retain the other witness records for later. “We spoke the daughter's name at dawn. / The feast grew still around it.”
- **Servants' refuge first:** escort threatened witnesses to shelter, delaying the public account. “We led the servants past the gate. / Their whispers crossed the fields.”

Carry: `cassandra-words`, `iphigenia-heard`, `atreid-witnesses-safe`. Leads to Libation Bearers and Eumenides. Iphigenia at Aulis supplies a recalled source strand; its selected account must match the fate asserted here. Red cloth changes from triumph to evidence to mourning.

## 6. Libation Bearers — Aeschylus

- Work: `aeschylus-libation-bearers`. Place: `greek-argos-tomb` (Agamemnon's Tomb). Account: `greek-atreid-account=aeschylus`.
- Time: the chosen tomb account. Existing hearing and refusal choices remain valid exits.
- Source: Electra meets Orestes at their father's tomb. Recognition joins their cause. Orestes kills Aegisthus and Clytemnestra, then sees the pursuing Furies. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/choephori.html).
- Poem question: What can a child carry from a murdered parent's grave?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Wine at the root.** Bring the libation vessel to the tomb. Ask which dead the household's prayer names. Offer: “The roots drink wine beneath the stone. / Whose thirst has brought us here?”
2. **The returned brother.** Compare the offered signs of kinship with shared memories and a witness's account. Offer: “A stranger knows the childhood door. / Let memory meet his face.”
3. **The hand at the threshold.** Bring the collected testimony to a hearing, support the revenge path, or carry mourners to refuge. Offer: “The father's name lies in their hands. / Choose what those hands shall carry.”

Branch endings:
- **Hearing, existing commitment:** the evidence travels under named custody. “We lifted witness from the grave. / The living heard the dead man's claim.”
- **Revenge, new source-aligned route:** Orestes acts and the player carries the aftermath toward Delphi. “The house drank blood for blood at dusk. / At dawn, the road heard footsteps.”
- **Refusal, existing commitment:** protect mourners and decline the revenge pledge. “We left the blade beside the stone. / The mourners walked with us.”

Carry: `atreid-testimony-custody`, `atreid-response`, `mother-claim-heard`. Eumenides branches according to what actually happened. A hearing path carries a disputed murder claim; a revenge path carries Orestes's killing and pursuit. The poem names each cause precisely.

## 7. Eumenides — Aeschylus

- Work: `aeschylus-eumenides`. Entry place: `greek-delphi` (Delphic Temple). Proposed continuation: `greek-athens` (Athenian Agora), with a named court scene.
- Time: aftermath of the selected Atreid path. A source-aligned revenge account follows Orestes from Delphi to the hearing. Earlier hearing paths use a clearly labeled adapted case.
- Source: the Furies pursue Orestes. Athena establishes a court; the tied vote frees him. She gives the Furies a place of honor within the city. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aeschylus/eumendides.html).
- Poem question: Can a city make room for the claim that loses its case?

Three proposed quests:
1. **The stained threshold.** Secure a temporary refuge at Delphi. Write down both the pursuer's claim and the refugee's reply. Offer: “The threshold holds a fleeing man. / The road still carries his pursuers.”
2. **Stones in the bowl.** Bring named evidence and witnesses to the Athenian court. Choose how the claims enter the hearing. Offer: “Each stone will strike the hollow bowl. / First give each voice its hour.”
3. **A seat for anger.** After judgment, negotiate where the aggrieved claim will live in the city's rites, safeguards, and memory. Offer: “The bowl is still; the anger walks. / Set out a place beside us.”

Branch endings:
- **Shared civic settlement:** accept judgment and endow a place for the pursuers' claim. “We gave old anger ground and fire. / Its watch became the city's own.”
- **Verdict with a living dissent:** publish the losing case and promise a later public review. “The stones were counted in the light. / One voice kept watch beside them.”
- **Restitution pact:** agree on concrete care for affected households, with each party's consent. This is an invented result. “We set bread where the judgment fell. / The living shared its weight.”

Carry: `court-claims-heard`, `fury-place`, `household-restitution`. These alter Wasps and Assemblywomen: jurors can quote an actual case; civic plans must fund an actual pledge. The source's view of motherhood can be openly challenged through added testimony; the adaptation owns that intervention.

## 8. Ajax — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-ajax`. Place: `greek-greek-camp` (The Greek Camp at Troy).
- Time: a named Trojan account. The proposed entry follows Ajax's death and centers the burial dispute.
- Source: Ajax's humiliation and divine delusion lead to catastrophe and his death. Teucer seeks burial against the commanders' opposition; Odysseus supports burial for his former rival. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/ajax.html).
- Poem question: Does an enemy's body still belong beneath the earth?

Three proposed quests:
1. **The child's shield.** Bring Ajax's shield to Tecmessa and Eurysaces. Let the household choose its place in the rite. Offer: “A child stands in his father's shade. / Carry the shield with care.”
2. **A rival's word.** Bring Teucer's burial request to Odysseus. Ask him to speak before the commanders. Offer: “The camp remembers every quarrel. / Ask what a rival owes.”
3. **Earth beside the ships.** Gather cloth and grave tools. Choose a public rite, a household rite, or an appeal backed by the camp's witnesses. Offer: “The ships face home; the dead lie here. / Give earth a human name.”

Branch endings:
- **Public burial through an enemy's plea:** “His rival spoke; we broke the ground. / The camp laid down its quarrel.”
- **Quiet household burial:** “We covered him beneath the stars. / His child held fast the shield.”
- **Public appeal:** keep a vigil while witnesses press the command for a rite; close only when a burial occurs. “We kept the lamps beside his feet. / At dawn, the camp brought earth.”

Carry: `ajax-burial-witness`, `enemy-mercy`, `tecmessa-household`. This answers the opening Antigone stanza. If the player once reported Antigone, the chorus can ask what changed. Odysseus's later request in Philoctetes carries this recorded act of mercy and his part in abandonment.

## 9. Antigone — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-antigone`. Work place: `greek-gate`; live quest sites also `greek-courtyard`, `greek-well`, `greek-palace`, `greek-battlefield`.
- Time: present opening. Keep `greek-theban-response` values `burial`, `concession`, `refusal`, and `exposure`.
- Source: Antigone buries her brother against Creon's decree. The clash reaches family, city, and divine law. Creon's late change comes after terrible losses. The current permission, withdrawal, and report paths are CosyWorld adaptations. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/antigone.html).
- Poem question: What will this household ask of the stranger it has fed?

Three existing quests become one three-part opening:
1. **A place at the table.** Return Dione's water jar. Offer: “Dione’s jar waits by the well. / The bread is warm within.” Completion: “I set the water by her hands. / She made room beside the bread.”
2. **Before the burial.** Hear the decree; choose the response; perform the rite on both burial routes. Offer: “Her brother lies beyond the gate. / The earth awaits a hand.” Permission-stage verse: “Creon has granted this one grave. / Her brother waits for earth.”
3. **What the courtyard knows.** Return to Dione and share the chosen deed. Offer: “Dione sets a place for me. / What shall I bring beside the bread?”

Existing branch endings, after public sharing:
- **Burial in defiance:** “I gave her brother to the earth. / Dione heard the deed I chose.”
- **Permission and completed rite:** “Creon’s leave became a grave. / Dione heard the deed I chose.”
- **Withdrawal:** “I chose to leave the burial. / Dione heard the choice I made.”
- **Report to the guard:** “I gave the guard the hidden plan. / Dione heard the choice I made.”

Carry: the existing response, burial-performed flag, and public-sharing flag; add the exact witnessed stanza only after its event. Permission alone leaves the rite stanza open. Each result may seed a later act of aid, defense, or repair. The returning table is the epic's measure: who eats there, who waits outside, whose voice enters its song.

## 10. Women of Trachis — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-women-of-trachis`. Place: `greek-trachis` (House at Trachis). Existing nearby site: `greek-oeta` (The Oeta Road).
- Time: named recalled household scene, or explicit alternate account if the gift's fate changes.
- Source: Deianira sends Heracles a robe treated with what she believes is a love charm. A remnant of wool reveals its destructive force. The robe causes terrible suffering. The play ends with Heracles's demand to be taken toward Oeta. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/trachinae.html).
- Poem question: What does love owe the person who receives its gift?

Three proposed quests:
1. **The captive's name.** Bring a meal to Iole. Hear how the herald describes her and ask what she wishes to share. Offer: “A stranger sits among the spoils. / Give her a place and name.”
2. **Wool in the sun.** Trace the charm's origin. Put aside the treated wool as evidence before the robe travels. Offer: “A gift lies folded from the light. / Ask what its threads remember.”
3. **The road after the gift.** Choose a new gift after the warning, recover the parcel on the road, or tend the harm in the source-aligned recalled scene. Offer: “The road has taken up the cloth. / Carry care along its length.”

Branch endings:
- **Gift changed, alternate account:** “We brought plain cloth into the light. / Her hands could choose the gift again.”
- **Parcel recovered, alternate account:** “We caught the robe before the shrine. / The road gave back its burden.”
- **Care after harm, source-aligned frame:** “We carried water toward the fire. / Each step bore what love had sent.”

Carry: `trachis-gift-evidence`, `iole-voice`, `oeta-care`. A recovered gift changes the chosen account explicitly. A source-aligned memory carries knowledge into later gift quests, including Medea. Cloth links Antigone's care, Agamemnon's display, and the harmful gift.

<a id="sophocles-oedipus-tyrannus"></a>

## 11. Oedipus Tyrannus — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-oedipus-tyrannus`. Place: `greek-palace` (Theban Palace Forecourt). Evidence route: `greek-cithaeron`.
- Time: recalled inquiry, named “Before the crown broke.” The present city already knows the family's broad fate.
- Source: a city's crisis drives the inquiry into Laius's death. Witnesses connect the king's origins to the old crime and his household. Oedipus discovers his identity. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/oedipus.html).
- Poem question: How should a city carry a truth that wounds its ruler and its children?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Names at the well.** Carry aid to affected households and record whom the public inquiry has heard. Offer: “The well reflects a weary crown. / Fill cups before you seek its shadow.”
2. **The shepherd's path.** Escort the surviving witness from the slopes. Keep the old man's words distinct from rumor. Offer: “An old man knows the mountain path. / Walk slowly with his story.”
3. **The opened record.** Choose how the verified account reaches the household and the city. Offer: “The names have met upon one stone. / Choose who shall hear them first.”

Branch endings:
- **Public account with care:** provide attendants for the family and speak the evidence openly. “We laid the names before the crowd. / Then held the children through the dusk.”
- **Household first:** tell the family with support, then hold a public hearing. “We spoke beside the family fire. / At dawn, the square received the truth.”
- **Witness-led account:** let the shepherd and other witnesses choose the form of their testimony. “The shepherd set his staff upright. / His voice outlived the crown.”

Carry: `theban-truth-custody`, `shepherd-protected`, `royal-children-care`. Links to Colonus, Phoenician Women, Antigone, and Clouds. These branches govern speech and care; the known parentage remains part of the recalled past.

## 12. Electra — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-electra`. Place: `greek-mycenae-palace` (Mycenaean Palace). Account: `greek-atreid-account=sophocles`.
- Time: chosen palace account. The existing hearing and refusal choices remain valid.
- Source: Electra grieves and waits for Orestes. A false death report and an urn deepen her grief before the siblings recognize each other. Their plot brings revenge against Clytemnestra and Aegisthus. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/electra.html).
- Poem question: What happens when hope itself becomes a tool of revenge?

Three proposed quests:
1. **A meal at the palace edge.** Bring Electra food. Carry a message between her and Chrysothemis under their chosen terms. Offer: “She keeps her watch beside the door. / Bring bread to grief's long vigil.”
2. **The empty urn.** Check the tutor's story against the sealed object and the claimant's own account. Choose when to tell Electra. Offer: “An urn has crossed the road at dawn. / Ask whose grief it carries.”
3. **The brother at the door.** Support recognition, then take the testimony to a hearing, aid the revenge plot, or escort those who leave it. Offer: “The brother stands within her reach. / Their hands must choose the hour.”

Branch endings:
- **Hearing, existing commitment:** “We set the urn before the court. / Its emptiness became a voice.”
- **Revenge, new source-aligned route:** “The brother crossed the palace sill. / Her long cry followed him inside.”
- **Refusal, existing commitment:** “We carried bread beyond the walls. / Her grief came walking with us.”

Carry: `palace-urn-truth`, `electra-hope-used`, `atreid-response`. The urn is specific to this account. Keep it separate from the tomb account's recognition signs and Euripides's farmhouse evidence. Any later court or Orestes chapter names the chosen source path.

<a id="sophocles-philoctetes"></a>

## 13. Philoctetes — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-philoctetes`. Place: `greek-lemnos` (The Lemnian Cave).
- Time: selected Trojan-war account. Travel establishes whether the bow's military need is still live or recalled.
- Source: Odysseus urges Neoptolemus to win Philoctetes's bow through deception. Neoptolemus later returns it. Heracles appears and directs the journey to Troy, where healing is promised. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/philoct.html).
- Poem question: Can a wounded man's consent survive an army's need?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Water at the cave.** Gather the plant Philoctetes uses for relief. Bring fresh water before discussing the bow. Offer: “A bow hangs where the wound keeps watch. / Bring water to the cave.”
2. **The borrowed bow.** Hold the bow during a bout of pain. Decide whether to return it at once or carry out the army's deception. Offer: “He places hunger in our hands. / The bow remembers whom it feeds.”
3. **The offered sail.** State the army's purpose and offer the chosen destination: Troy, home, or continued shelter. Offer: “The ship has room for one more voice. / Ask where its owner sails.”

Branch endings:
- **Truth and a freely accepted journey to Troy:** “We gave the bow back to his hand. / He chose the sail toward Troy.”
- **Chosen homecoming, alternate result:** “He named his home; we turned the sail. / The bow lay quiet at his feet.”
- **Deception followed by repair:** return the stolen bow, confess, and accept his chosen terms for further contact. “We brought the bow back through the rain. / He set the price of trust.”

Carry: `lemnos-consent`, `bow-returned`, `wound-care`. A betrayal closes quick passage until repair occurs. Links to Ajax's burial stanza and the costs of command at Troy. Care builds trust. Philoctetes keeps his choice and his bow.

<a id="sophocles-oedipus-at-colonus"></a>

## 14. Oedipus at Colonus — Sophocles

- Work: `sophocles-oedipus-at-colonus`. Place: `greek-colonus` (Colonus Grove).
- Time: recalled refuge before the live Theban aftermath. Its memory supplies a present custom of sanctuary.
- Source: Oedipus reaches Colonus with Antigone. Theseus grants protection. Creon uses force against the daughters; Polynices seeks his father's support. Oedipus's final resting place becomes a guarded secret entrusted to Theseus. [Primary text](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/colonus.html).
- Poem question: Can a city welcome an exile while leaving his last choice his own?

Three proposed quests:
1. **Water for the grove.** Fetch the ritual water and learn the sanctuary's customs from a local guide. Offer: “An old man rests beneath the leaves. / Bring water with a gentle step.”
2. **The daughters' road.** Carry word to Theseus when the daughters are seized. Help arrange their safe return. Offer: “Two paths run outward from the grove. / Bring the daughters home together.”
3. **The kept place.** Witness the promise of protection. Choose who receives the public account of the refuge and preserve the entrusted resting-place secret. Offer: “The roots will keep an old man's rest. / Choose how his welcome lives.”

Branch endings:
- **Living sanctuary custom:** households pledge aid to the next exile. “We kept his place beneath the leaves. / The next exile found a chair.”
- **Family remembrance:** give the daughters a place to tell what care cost them. “His daughters spoke beneath the trees. / Their voices held the road.”
- **Public pact:** record the city's protection promise and the duties it imposes on rulers. “We carved the welcome by the gate. / The king stood subject to its words.”

Carry: `colonus-refuge-promise`, `daughters-returned`, `resting-place-kept`. Links to Suppliant Women, Children of Heracles, and the final return to Dione. The present Antigone can recall that she once guided her father, which makes the brother's exposed body part of a life of care.

## Links that let these strands become one poem

| Carried thing | First use | Later change | Final question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water | Dione's household task | Burial, refuge, wound care, offerings | Whose cup will the homecoming fill? |
| Cloth | Funeral care | Royal display, harmful gift, poor shelter | Who has been covered, honored, or harmed? |
| Bread | Welcome at Dione's table | Support for exiles, mourners, and survivors | Who has earned a place through care? |
| Name | Brother, witness, captive | Enemy dead, hidden kin, public record | Whose name will the finished song carry? |
| Fire | Household warmth | War beacon, stolen gift, trial rite | Which fires can people gather around? |
| Vessel | Water jar | False urn, vote bowl, libation cup | What has each container made the city hold? |

Suggested tragic order within the larger poem: Antigone opening → recalled Theban roots (Oedipus Tyrannus, Colonus, Seven Against Thebes) → refuge and care (Suppliant Women, Trachis) → war's human claims (Philoctetes, Ajax, Persians) → one Atreid account (Agamemnon and Libation Bearers, or Sophocles Electra, or the Euripides path) → Eumenides → Prometheus as a late question about the gifts and power behind the whole journey.
