# Euripides: nineteen strands for the growing poem

[Read the full poem plan and chapter map](README.md).

Research and original design, 26 October 2026. The work IDs and sites below match `v2/content/greek-drama/card-world.json`. Quest actions, player branches, and every verse below are new CosyWorld writing. Each source note describes the play. Each design section proposes an adaptation. The poem records the chosen action and its cost.

## Shared rules

Each chapter has three quests. Each earned line is at most twelve words. A final two-line stanza records the chosen result. Keep a stable first-person voice for the traveller. Named speakers can answer in their own voice. A slash in a verse marks a line break.

A remembered scene has a clear entrance: a witness, song, or object opens an earlier tale. It shows the past that belongs to the current path. An alternate telling has its own named page. This lets the traveller hear all nineteen plays while the people in each path retain one past. When a player changes a cause, later scenes follow that changed cause.

Use objects as links: water, cloth, a cup, an oath, a name, a bowl of earth, a letter, a sail. Record who gave the object and who suffered for it. A poem line can recall that history: the cup carried from Pherae means something different in Corinth or Delphi.

## E01. Alcestis — The Guest and the Empty Chair

**Work:** `euripides-alcestis` · **Site:** `greek-pherae`, Palace of Pherae · **Frame:** a separate earlier household tale; Heracles appears before the disaster in *Heracles*.

**Source ground.** Alcestis dies in Admetus's place. He receives Heracles while hiding the household's grief. A servant reveals the loss. Heracles brings Alcestis back. The bargain, the burden of hospitality, and the husband's debt give this play its tension. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/alcestis.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **An Empty Chair:** prepare the table with the servant; set aside Alcestis's place. Earn: “I set a cup beside her empty chair.”
2. **The Guest Must Hear:** tell Heracles whose funeral the house is holding, or help Admetus keep the feast's appearance. Earn openly: “I gave our guest the name beneath the silence.” Earn concealment: “I poured the wine above the muffled weeping.”
3. **The Veiled Return:** if the guest knows, meet him at the grave and receive the returned woman; otherwise help the household bury her and preserve her wishes. Earn return: “I held the door while she came through the dusk.” Earn mourning: “I laid her wishes where the children sleep.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Return with a debt:** Alcestis lives; Admetus must grant her time, care, and a voice in the household. “Death loosed one hand; the other held our promise. / Her chair stood full, and all our lives owed room.”
- **The household bears the loss:** funeral completed; the children receive a lasting place and provision. “We carried bread into the days she left us. / Her name became the work of every dawn.”

**Carry:** hospitality either shelters grief or conceals it. Heracles later remembers the traveller's truthfulness or silence. Both branches give a usable table motif. The return belongs to this one divine bargain.

## E02. Medea — The Gifts at the Door

**Work:** `euripides-medea` · **Site:** `greek-corinth`, Corinthian Courtyard · **Frame:** a present household strand, before an Athenian refuge is settled.

**Source ground.** Jason abandons Medea for a royal marriage. Creon orders her exile. She gains a day's delay and Aegeus's promise of refuge. Her gifts kill the bride and Creon; she also kills her children. The play sets betrayal and exile beside her terrible choice. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/medea.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **One Day of Shelter:** secure the promised day; provide food and hear the nurse's fears. Earn: “I bought one dawn beneath a borrowed roof.”
2. **An Oath Beyond Corinth:** witness Aegeus's pledge and arrange the household's passage. Earn: “I carried hope inside a stranger's oath.”
3. **The Bridal Gifts:** inspect the gifts with the nurse; choose whether to carry them, expose them, or use the delay for a witnessed departure. Earn exposure: “I laid the fatal gifts before the gate.” Earn departure: “I led the children down the waking road.” Earn complicity: “I bore the bride a gleaming mouth of grief.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Refuge with witnesses:** departure requires the nurse's help, safe transport, and Medea's agreement. Jason keeps his place; the exiles carry the cost. “Her anger crossed the sea among the living. / The city kept its crown; we kept their names.”
- **Revenge and loss:** the gifts pass; Medea's violence follows as witnessed tragedy. The traveller helps the nurse name and mourn the children. “The gold shone once; the house grew dark around it. / I carried small names farther than the flames.”
- **Public reckoning:** expose the plan and bring the threatened children into witnessed shelter. Royal power controls the hearing; Medea faces exile under guard. “I raised the cloth; the whole street saw its poison. / The truth won daylight, and the guards drew near.”

**Carry:** the oath and children's names change later refuge scenes in *Children of Heracles*, *Ion*, and *Suppliant Women*. These rescue and hearing branches are deliberate new alternatives; Medea remains a person who must choose.

## E03. Children of Heracles — The Price of Shelter

**Work:** `euripides-children-of-heracles` · **Site:** `greek-marathon`, Marathon Altar · **Frame:** a later generation after Heracles; separate from the children who die in his Theban household.

**Source ground.** Iolaus and Heracles's children seek protection from Eurystheus. Demophon receives them. An oracle demands a noble maiden's sacrifice; a daughter of Heracles volunteers. Iolaus joins the battle. The captive Eurystheus faces Alcmene's demand for death. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/heracleidae.html). Name the volunteer “Heracles's daughter” in the source-facing text; the surviving play leaves her unnamed.

**Three quests.**

1. **The Altar Circle:** bring water and stand with the children against the herald's claim. Earn: “I filled their cups inside the altar's shadow.”
2. **Who Pays for Shelter:** hear the daughter herself; bring the oracle before the town; choose the sacrifice path or organize a costly evacuation. Earn debate: “I made the city hear the price of shelter.”
3. **The Captive King:** after battle, guard Eurystheus through a hearing or yield him to Alcmene; after evacuation, escort the last family beyond pursuit. Earn hearing: “I brought the hunter living to the judgment.” Earn vengeance: “I heard the old grief claim its living payment.” Earn evacuation: “I counted every child beyond the ridge.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Victory and sacrifice:** the source-shaped route honours the daughter's choice and records the town's dependence on it. “The city stood; one place lay bare beside us. / We owed its peace to one young mortal life.”
- **Shelter on the road:** an invented refuge route spends provisions and leaves the town exposed to political anger. “Our shelter moved beneath a weathered blanket. / The road grew long enough to hold them all.”
- **Victory and mercy:** after the costly battle, protect the captive for a public decision; Alcmene's anger remains. “I set the fallen hunter inside our circle. / The children watched the hand that held the gate.”

**Carry:** count named people in their care and living witnesses. Later towns answer the traveller's earlier choices toward strangers and prisoners.

## E04. Hippolytus — The Sealed Word

**Work:** `euripides-hippolytus` · **Site:** `greek-troezen`, Troezen Palace · **Frame:** present household tale; keep this Theseus's private grief distinct from his public acts elsewhere.

**Source ground.** Aphrodite drives Phaedra's desire for Hippolytus. The nurse reveals it under an oath. Phaedra's death and false accusation move Theseus to curse his son. Artemis reveals the truth; father and dying son meet in grief. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/hippolytus.html); [complete closing scene](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/euripides-hippolytus-sb/).

**Three quests.**

1. **Water for the Sickroom:** aid Phaedra; hear the nurse privately. Earn: “I cooled the cloth upon her burning brow.”
2. **The Sealed Word:** bring the accusation to a witnessed hearing and ask Hippolytus what his oath permits him to say. Earn: “I set the sealed word down beside its witness.”
3. **Before the Sea Answers:** ask Theseus to delay his curse; if it has passed, meet the wounded son and summon his father. Earn delay: “I held one hour between the word and thunder.” Earn grief: “I brought the father where his son lay breathing.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **A hearing before punishment:** an invented branch requires the nurse's testimony and Theseus's consent; shame and divine anger remain. “The sea lay still beneath a father's silence. / A living son stood waiting for his name.”
- **Truth after the curse:** the source-shaped grief route ends in the son's chosen forgiveness. “The sea gave back the body of his judgment. / A son gave mercy with his final breath.”

**Carry:** evidence and oaths alter the traveller's stance in *Ion*, *Orestes*, and the courts of *Wasps*. Keep this myth’s accusation and evidence specific to its named people and events.

## E05. Andromache — The Child at the Shrine

**Work:** `euripides-andromache` · **Site:** `greek-thetis`, Thetis's Sanctuary · **Frame:** after Troy; depends on Andromache's captive fate or is heard as an alternate telling.

**Source ground.** Andromache, enslaved in Neoptolemus's household, seeks sanctuary. Hermione and Menelaus threaten her and her son. Peleus intervenes. Orestes draws Hermione away; news comes of Neoptolemus's death at Delphi. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/andromache.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **The Shrine's Threshold:** carry food to Andromache and learn where her child is held. Earn: “I brought a mother's hunger to the holy threshold.”
2. **Fetch the Old Defender:** bring Peleus and witnesses before Menelaus removes them. Earn: “I brought an old man's staff against their weapons.”
3. **Two Roads from the House:** choose protection through Peleus's household or help mother and child leave under a public pledge. Receive the Delphic messenger either way. Earn stay: “I set their names inside the household's keeping.” Earn leave: “I walked beside the child beyond the boundary.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Protected within the house:** immediate safety rests on an aging defender; later power remains uncertain. “An old hand held the doorway through the shouting. / The child slept while the palace counted heirs.”
- **A road of their own:** invented departure costs rank, wealth, and a known shelter; their ties to Troy endure. “Her child learned home from footsteps at her shoulder. / The road received the names the house had weighed.”

**Carry:** connect the Trojan farewell to this mother's later account. A warning sent from Delphi may change how Neoptolemus dies in an explicit alternate path; the default messenger preserves the play's loss.

## E06. Hecuba — The Gold Beneath the Tide

**Work:** `euripides-hecuba` · **Site:** `greek-thrace`, The Thracian Shore · **Frame:** immediately after Troy, before the captive ships leave.

**Source ground.** Polyxena is sacrificed at Achilles's tomb. Hecuba then learns that Polymestor killed her son Polydorus for his gold. She secures room for revenge, blinds Polymestor, and kills his sons. The play joins mourning, broken guest trust, and retaliatory cruelty. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/hecuba.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **The Daughter's Farewell:** hear Polyxena's own words and prepare the cloth for her rites. Earn: “I held the cloth beneath her steady voice.”
2. **What the Tide Returns:** recover Polydorus's body; trace the entrusted gold. Earn: “The tide laid broken guest trust at my feet.”
3. **The King's Visit:** carry Hecuba's summons into a revenge trap or gather witnesses for a public charge; keep the children's fate explicit. Earn charge: “I laid the stolen gold before the gathered captives.” Earn trap: “I led the golden liar toward the tent.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Revenge:** source-shaped ending; Hecuba gains vengeance and adds children to the dead. “Her grief took eyes, and other mothers' children. / The sea kept moving past our sharpened sorrow.”
- **A charge before witnesses:** invented hearing recovers names and proof; the army may grant burial while protecting its ally. “We named the gold, the guest, the hand that killed him. / The judges heard; the tide still bore our dead.”
- **Burial and departure:** bury both children and entrust evidence to another traveller; the ruler keeps his wealth for now. “I gave her children earth beside the water. / Their names sailed farther than the king's bright gold.”

**Carry:** the names can join the courtyard chorus; guest trust echoes *Alcestis*, *Cyclops*, and *Suppliant Women*.

## E07. Suppliant Women — Earth for Every Son

**Work:** `euripides-suppliant-women` · **Site:** `greek-eleusis`, Eleusinian Sanctuary · **Frame:** after the attack on Thebes; directly recalls the opening burial choice.

**Source ground.** The mothers of the Argive dead ask Athens to recover their sons' bodies from Thebes. Theseus acts after debate. Burial follows, alongside Evadne's death on Capaneus's pyre and the prospect of another generation's vengeance. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/suppliants.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **The Mothers' Branches:** carry their plea from Aethra to Theseus. Earn: “I carried mothers' branches through the crowded square.”
2. **The Burial Road:** seek a witnessed return of the bodies; if refused, join the recovery after the city chooses force. Earn truce: “I brought the fallen home beneath a truce.” Earn force: “I walked behind the shields that bore the fallen.”
3. **Beside the Pyre:** name the dead; stay with Evadne and Iphis; ask the children what they wish carried forward. Earn care: “I stayed beside the living through the fire.” Earn aftermath: “I held her father's hand beside the ashes.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **A common burial pledge:** invented diplomacy draws on Thebes's earlier burial precedent. “The earth received the sons of either banner. / Our living hands held room for one another.”
- **Recovery by force:** burial is won through war; the children inherit both grief and a renewed claim. “We took their bodies home behind our spears. / Their children learned the weight of both together.”
- **The pyre's second loss:** source-shaped Evadne outcome adds an intimate stanza to either recovery route. “The flame took one more voice from those who mourned. / Her father carried silence through the dawn.”

**Carry:** the opening's decree, permission, defiance, or abandonment becomes quoted public memory. Future peace chapters must answer the next generation's claim.

## E08. Electra — The Farmhouse Door

**Work:** `euripides-electra` · **Site:** `greek-argive-farm`, The Argive Farmhouse · **Frame:** alternate Atreid telling; current gate `greek-atreid-account=euripides`.

**Source ground.** Electra lives with a poor farmer who treats her with care. Orestes returns. Their revenge kills Aegisthus and Clytemnestra; grief follows the deed. The farmhouse brings royal claims into the scale of food, labour, and household dignity. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/electra_eur.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **Water Before Kings:** share Electra's water work and the farmer's meal. Earn: “I carried royal grief inside a water jar.”
2. **The Brother's Mark:** test the returning stranger's identity with the old servant; listen to the household's account. Earn: “I found a brother in the old man's witness.”
3. **The Mother's Visit:** carry the false childbirth summons, arrange a truthful meeting, or help the siblings leave. Earn revenge: “I called their mother through a borrowed promise.” Earn hearing: “I opened the poor house to every claim.” Earn exile: “I closed the gate upon their father's weapons.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **The revenge fulfilled:** source-shaped killings give the siblings exile and grief. “The house gained blood enough to crown its children. / They stood beside their mother, smaller than their names.”
- **The claim spoken:** invented hearing preserves testimony and exposes the siblings to Aegisthus's power. “Her mother spoke; the small room held both sorrows. / The crown still cast its shadow through the door.”
- **A chosen exile:** leave with the farmer's aid; the royal claim remains unresolved. “We took the road with bread the farmer gave us. / Their father's crown stayed heavy on the hill.”

**Carry:** the opening's jar returns with a changed meaning. Keep this Electra's home and recognition distinct from the Aeschylus and Sophocles versions.

## E09. Heracles — The Friend Beside the Ruin

**Work:** `euripides-heracles` · **Site:** `greek-courtyard`, Theban Courtyard · **Frame:** earlier Theban tale; enters through a household memory, with its own cast and time.

**Source ground.** Lycus threatens Heracles's family. Heracles returns and defeats him. Divine madness then makes Heracles kill his own wife and children. Theseus offers friendship and a home in Athens after the disaster. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/heracles.html); [complete play](https://chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/euripides-herakles/).

**Three quests.**

1. **The Altar and the Children:** bring food and send word for aid while Lycus holds power. Earn: “I brought their bread beneath the tyrant's torches.”
2. **After the Hero's Return:** warn neighbours and prepare a place of care as the household collapses. Earn: “I gathered neighbours where the great doors shattered.”
3. **A Hand for the Living:** help Theseus reach Heracles, or stay with Amphitryon for the burials while another companion escorts him. Earn journey: “I lent my shoulder to the broken strong man.” Earn vigil: “I stayed to name the children with their grandfather.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Walk with Heracles:** the traveller gives care on the road to Athens; another trusted person holds the funeral duty. “His strength had carried beasts from death's deep doorway. / A friend's small hand now helped him face the dawn.”
- **Stay with the household:** the traveller keeps the dead visible while Theseus carries the living forward. “The road took him; I stayed beside their names. / Each dawn I brought his father bread and water.”

**Carry:** *Alcestis* can make Heracles a former benefactor. The later scene must name his wife and children as people. Keep the disaster in this chapter's source-shaped core; the branch concerns lasting care and duty.

## E10. Trojan Women — What the Ships Will Carry

**Work:** `euripides-trojan-women` · **Site:** `greek-troy`, The Trojan Walls · **Frame:** the fall of Troy; Helen's scene uses the chosen Troy/Egypt telling.

**Source ground.** Captive women learn where the victors will send them. Cassandra and Andromache face removal. Astyanax is killed, and Hecuba prepares his burial upon Hector's shield. The city burns. Helen and Hecuba argue before Menelaus. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/troj_women.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **Names Before the Ships:** gather the women's names, destinations, and messages for kin. Earn: “I wrote their names where ships had written numbers.”
2. **The Shield as Cradle:** help Hecuba wash and bury Astyanax; or, in an explicit changed-war path, bring him into witnessed refuge before the order takes effect. Earn burial: “I washed a child's small body on his father's shield.” Earn refuge: “I hid a living heartbeat beneath the shield.”
3. **The Last City Song:** choose which witness speaks the departure song; distribute the names among separate ships. Earn: “I shared their names among the parting sails.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **The city's witness:** source-shaped losses endure; the women carry one another's accounts. “The towers fell; their voices crossed the water. / I held a city in the names they gave me.”
- **A child carried out:** an invented branch needs shelter and a willing crew; it changes Andromache's later household. “The city burned around a hidden breathing. / One shield bore home the life it once defended.”
- **A captive family stays together:** bargain transport space for kin at the cost of other cargo and the traveller's passage. “I gave my berth to hands that clung together. / Their ship grew small; the shore remained beneath me.”

**Carry:** a named person or message should reach *Andromache*, *Hecuba*, or *Helen*. In the Egypt telling, treat Troy's claims about Helen as the speakers' beliefs; keep the phantom distinction clear.

## E11. Iphigenia in Tauris — The Letter in the Salt Wind

**Work:** `euripides-iphigenia-in-tauris` · **Site:** `greek-tauris`, The Taurian Temple · **Frame:** after the chosen Atreid trial/flight; requires a surviving Iphigenia in this telling.

**Source ground.** Iphigenia serves a temple where strangers face sacrifice. Orestes and Pylades arrive. Her letter leads to recognition. The reunited siblings plan escape with Artemis's image under a cleansing rite. Orestes speaks of a trial that left some Furies still pursuing him. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/iph_taur.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **One Stranger's Letter:** arrange for the priestess's message to reach home; hear it aloud with her leave. Earn: “I heard a sister's name inside the letter.”
2. **The Shared Childhood:** test remembered details before the ritual proceeds. Earn: “Their childhood crossed the years between their faces.”
3. **The Cleansing Shore:** prepare the rite and launch; choose who leaves, who holds the shore, and what happens to the sacred image. Earn flight: “I pushed their boat beyond the temple's shadow.” Earn pact: “I set the sacred image between our open hands.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **The ritual escape:** source-shaped deception makes escape possible; the local attendants bear its risks. “The salt washed blood's old claim from living shoulders. / Her letter folded home into the sail.”
- **A pact at the shore:** invented route uses local witnesses to exchange safe passage and a changed rite; requires the ruler's assent. “We gave the altar water from the sea. / Its keepers watched the strangers leave as guests.”
- **The keeper stays:** invented Iphigenia choice: send the men and her testimony home while she works with attendants to change the rite. “Her brother sailed beneath the morning star. / She kept the shore, and called the next ship welcome.”

**Carry:** her fate at Aulis must support this chapter. A prior death path opens it as a separate telling. Taking the image leaves a local claim that the returning poem must acknowledge.

## E12. Ion — The Basket and the Name

**Work:** `euripides-ion` · **Site:** `greek-delphi`, Delphic Temple · **Frame:** a separate earlier foundation tale, encountered through the sanctuary.

**Source ground.** Creusa's lost child serves Apollo's temple as Ion. Xuthus believes Ion is his son. Creusa, who bore Apollo's child after sexual violence, sees a threat to her household. A poisoning plot precedes recognition through the child's saved objects. [Primary text, early action](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/ion.html); [complete Coleridge translation, including the recognition and Athena's closing instruction](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Plays_of_Euripides_(Coleridge)/Ion).

**Three quests.**

1. **Sweep the Temple:** work with Ion; hear how a found child made a life there. Earn: “I swept the leaves beside the temple's foundling.”
2. **The Cup Set Aside:** stop the poisoned cup and hear Creusa's account before judgment. Earn: “I set the cup aside and heard his mother.”
3. **The Saved Basket:** bring the priestess's stored objects; let mother and son identify them and choose who hears their history. Earn: “I laid his first small clothes beneath the sunlight.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Private recognition:** protect mother and son's chosen privacy; the public household keeps an easier story. “She knew the stitch her younger hands had made. / The world heard one tale; their joined hands held another.”
- **A public maternal account:** invented branch gives Creusa control of her testimony and challenges the temple's authority. “She named the god beneath his own bright roof. / Her son stood near, and heard his mother whole.”
- **Ion chooses his household:** invented branch lets him remain with the temple carers while acknowledging Creusa. “He kept the broom that first had fed his hands. / His mother left a place for every home.”

**Carry:** family belongs to care, chosen ties, and known parentage. The basket can answer the jar, cradle, shield, and funeral cloth. The complete ending makes Xuthus's belief a deliberate public story; the proposed public maternal account changes that ending.

## E13. Helen — The Woman and the Name

**Work:** `euripides-helen` · **Site:** `greek-egypt-tomb`, Proteus's Tomb in Egypt · **Frame:** alternate war account; current gate `greek-helen-account=egypt`.

**Source ground.** Helen says a phantom went to Troy while she lived in Egypt. Menelaus arrives and struggles to accept this. The phantom's disappearance confirms the account. Helen and Menelaus win Theonoe's aid and use a staged sea burial to escape Theoclymenus. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/helen.html); [complete play](https://chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/euripides-helen/).

**Three quests.**

1. **The Tomb's Witness:** hear Helen and bring a second witness to the phantom's disappearance. Earn: “I heard the woman answer to her stolen name.”
2. **The Sister's Silence:** ask Theonoe for aid while acknowledging the danger to her. Earn: “A sister held our lives inside her silence.”
3. **The Empty Sea Burial:** prepare the voyage under the funeral claim, or seek a public hearing with Proteus's household witnesses. Earn escape: “I laid an empty shroud aboard the waiting boat.” Earn hearing: “I spoke her living name before the throne.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Escape together:** source-shaped plan; the sea receives an empty funeral and a living woman. “We mourned a living man to free his wife. / The wind took up the name that war had broken.”
- **Public testimony:** invented route risks the ruler's anger; if refuge holds, letters carry the correction before Helen sails. “Her body stood where all could hear her answer. / Her name set sail ahead of her that day.”
- **Helen chooses sanctuary:** invented route keeps her under a wider witnessed protection while Menelaus carries the account home. “She kept her ground beside the faithful tomb. / He sailed with truth more costly than his spoils.”

**Carry:** change blame verses in the war strand. The living Helen and the women of Troy each retain the truth of their own suffering.

## E14. Phoenician Women — The Mother's Truce

**Work:** `euripides-phoenician-women` · **Site:** `greek-palace`, Theban Palace Forecourt · **Frame:** remembered Theban war before the opening burial; or an explicit changed-history telling.

**Source ground.** Jocasta brings Eteocles and Polyneices to a failed meeting. Menoeceus offers himself after a prophecy. The brothers kill one another; Jocasta dies beside them. Antigone and the old Oedipus face the aftermath. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/phoenissae.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **Safe Passage Through the Gate:** escort Polyneices into the truce and hear both claims. Earn: “I led one brother through the other's gate.”
2. **The Price Named for Thebes:** hear Menoeceus and Creon; gather witnesses to the demand. Earn: “A young man's life lay under every shield.”
3. **The Mother Between Them:** carry Jocasta's appeal to the field; choose a witnessed shared rule proposal, or recover Jocasta's body after the duel and mark where each son has fallen. Earn truce: “I set their mother's bread between the brothers.” Earn aftermath: “I marked the brothers where their mother fell.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **The broken truce:** source-shaped history feeds *Before the Burial*. “Two brothers fell beneath one mother's shadow. / The gate stood whole; the house lay split within.”
- **A shared rule on trial:** invented alternate past requires both brothers' assent and hostages or public guarantees. It creates a new present. “Two hands held power for one uncertain season. / The city watched the bread they broke together.”
- **People through the gate:** the brothers choose war; the traveller uses the truce to move civilians to shelter. “Their banners met above the dusty road. / I counted living footsteps through the gate.”

**Carry:** in the main poem, the opening already fixes the brothers' deaths. The traveller marks their bodies outside the gate; the burial order still leaves Polyneices exposed. Recovering Jocasta's body preserves that opening. A changed past belongs to its own woven version and receives a different burial chapter. The Phoenician chorus carries the war's meaning beyond Thebes.

## E15. Orestes — The City at the Sickbed

**Work:** `euripides-orestes` · **Site:** `greek-argos-palace`, Argive Palace · **Frame:** a chosen continuation after the killing of his mother. Current content tags this work `greek-atreid-account=aeschylus`; retain that fact in migration planning. Give the poem a separate continuation choice.

**Source ground.** Orestes lies ill after killing his mother. A civic decision threatens him and Electra. Menelaus gives little help. Orestes, Electra, and Pylades plan further violence against Helen and Hermione. Apollo imposes a settlement at the end. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/orestes.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **Keep the Sickroom:** bring water, let Electra rest, and hear Orestes when he can speak. Earn: “I kept the lamp beside his broken sleep.”
2. **A Voice at the Assembly:** gather witnesses and carry a clear account to the city. Earn: “I took the house's sorrow to the square.”
3. **The Hostage at the Door:** release Hermione and seek a hearing, accept exile with safeguards, or join the desperate palace threat. Earn release: “I opened both my hands before the hostage.” Earn exile: “I carried water for the road from Argos.” Earn threat: “I stood where anger closed the palace doors.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Submit the claim:** invented hearing route admits the killing and asks for a public judgment; acquittal depends on the chosen legal strand. “The hand that killed lay open in the daylight. / The city had to answer with its own.”
- **Exile under witness:** invented guarded passage trades the royal claim for survival. “We left the crown beneath the shuttered windows. / The road asked only how we bore the dead.”
- **Violence answered by a god:** source-shaped crisis; Apollo's settlement gives closure by divine command and leaves human costs to the chorus. “A god spoke peace above the smoking doorway. / Below, the hostage's hands still shook in mine.”

**Carry:** requires the killing of his mother in this telling. A prior peaceful Electra result opens a new aftermath chapter or this play as an alternate account. Keep this continuation distinct from a completed Aeschylean reconciliation.

## E16. Bacchae — The Mountain Sees

**Work:** `euripides-bacchae` · **Site:** `greek-cithaeron`, Cithaeron Slopes · **Frame:** early Thebes, entered as a remembered divine-household tale.

**Source ground.** Pentheus tries to suppress Dionysus's worship, then follows the disguised god's plan to spy upon the women. His mother Agave and the other women kill him in their frenzy. Cadmus helps Agave recognize her son's remains. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/bacchan.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **The Worshippers' Door:** hear the detained worshippers and bring their account to the city. Earn: “I heard the mountain singing through the prison.”
2. **The Borrowed Robe:** decide whether to guide Pentheus's spying journey or seek a public meeting with the worshippers. Earn spying: “I held the robe that hid the watching king.” Earn meeting: “I brought the city's questions to the dancers.”
3. **What the Mother Carries:** after the source-shaped death, help Cadmus recover remains and speak gently with Agave; on the meeting path, guard the return road through the god's anger. Earn grief: “I helped her see the child within her trophy.” Earn return: “I kept the path between the drums and houses.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **The god's terrible victory:** source-shaped ruin; the chorus holds wonder and horror together. “The mountain gave her back the son she carried. / Her hands learned grief beneath the fading drums.”
- **Worship with a public boundary:** an explicit invented route requires Pentheus to yield control and the worshippers to choose terms. Dionysus still demands recognition. “The city opened room beneath its torches. / The mountain kept a darkness of its own.”

**Carry:** wine and celebration carry danger as well as release. Comedy can later answer this chapter's fear with freely chosen festivity. The poet records the god's power and the family's loss together.

## E17. Iphigenia at Aulis — The Wind's Price

**Work:** `euripides-iphigenia-at-aulis` · **Site:** `greek-aulis`, Aulis Anchorage · **Frame:** remembered departure for Troy; choose the telling before its effects enter the Atreid and Taurian strands.

**Source ground.** Agamemnon summons Iphigenia under a false marriage promise. The stalled army demands a sacrifice. Achilles offers help. Iphigenia's eventual decision and the transmitted rescue ending sit within a difficult textual history. [Primary play, early action](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/iphi_aul.html); [edition discussion of the ending](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037d-7090-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/download). The received ending's wording and authorship are disputed; label the selected telling.

**Three quests.**

1. **The Second Letter:** carry Agamemnon's attempted recall and find who intercepts it. Earn: “I bore a father's doubt through sleeping soldiers.”
2. **The Wedding's True Name:** tell mother and daughter the purpose of the summons; secure Achilles's witness. Earn: “I tore the wedding veil from what they asked.”
3. **A Way from the Altar:** prepare an escape with consenting allies, witness the sacrifice, or follow the reported divine rescue telling. Earn escape: “I led her down the path behind the ships.” Earn sacrifice: “I held her mother's hand beside the altar.” Earn rescue: “I heard the hind cry where the girl had stood.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **The army yields its price:** invented escape or public resistance; commanders lose unity, and the war's timing changes. “The sails hung still above a living daughter. / The captains learned the weight of waiting wind.”
- **The sacrifice remembered:** tragic telling; preserves the grievance that enters Agamemnon's homecoming. “The wind rose through the cloth her mother held. / Each sail went white above a debt of blood.”
- **Artemis carries her away:** received rescue telling; the fleet believes one account while Tauris will reveal another. “The altar held a beast beneath the knife. / A daughter's road bent farther than the fleet.”

**Carry:** this is a major loom knot. A living escape changes the revenge family's claims. A Taurian priestess requires the rescue telling or a clearly authored later journey. Record what each person believes separately from the chosen truth.

## E18. Rhesus — The Watchword in the Dark

**Work:** `euripides-rhesus` · **Site:** `greek-trojan-camp`, The Trojan Night Camp · **Frame:** the war before Troy's fall; present as a named night tale. The play survives under Euripides's name; its authorship is disputed.

**Source ground.** Rhesus arrives with Thracian allies. Dolon scouts the Greek camp. Odysseus and Diomedes enter the Trojan camp; Rhesus dies in the night raid. A wounded charioteer brings his account, and a Muse mourns her son. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/rhesus.html); [Perseus catalogue note on attribution](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0004%3Aalphabetic+letter%3DE%3Aentry+group%3D7%3Aentry%3Deuripides).

**Three quests.**

1. **Find the New Camp:** guide the exhausted allies to their ground and check the watch. Earn: “I set a lamp beside the strangers' horses.”
2. **Test the Night's Word:** challenge travellers at the boundary; compare the watchword with the sentries' account. Earn: “I asked the dark to give its rightful name.”
3. **The Wounded Charioteer:** choose an early warning and evacuation, or treat the survivor after the raid and bring his testimony to Hector. Earn warning: “I woke the sleeping camp before the hoofbeats.” Earn witness: “I bound the wound and carried what he saw.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **A warning heeded:** invented route saves Rhesus for a changed war episode; the raiders escape or face pursuit. “The horses stirred before the blades came near. / One spoken word gave dawn another shape.”
- **The raid's witness:** source-shaped death; evidence prevents a false charge against the hosts from becoming the only account. “His mother sang above the stolen horses. / I kept one wounded voice against the rumour.”

**Carry:** failed or honoured guest protection echoes Hecuba's Thracian shore. A rescued Rhesus needs a later fate before the poem returns to the fall of Troy.

## E19. Cyclops — The Guest Who Became a Meal

**Work:** `euripides-cyclops` · **Site:** `greek-sicily`, The Sicilian Cave · **Frame:** a homecoming sea tale. Keep the satyr chorus's earthy comedy beside the captive household's danger.

**Source ground.** Odysseus and his crew meet Silenus and the satyrs, who serve the Cyclops. The host eats travellers. Wine and a false name enable a violent escape; the satyrs' boasts exceed their courage. [Primary play](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/cyclops.html).

**Three quests.**

1. **Milk for a Stranger:** seek provisions; learn who owns the food and who labours for it. Earn: “I asked for bread; the cave weighed out my body.”
2. **The Cup Goes Round:** obtain the wine and make a practical escape pact with the satyrs. Earn: “I poured the wine while brave men found excuses.”
3. **Beyond the Cave:** help the crew's source-shaped attack, arrange a risky escape while the host sleeps, or spend the opening to release captive workers first. Earn attack: “I gripped the wood beside the shaking sailors.” Earn flight: “I led our feet beyond his sleeping thunder.” Earn shared escape: “I held the path while every captive passed.”

**Branches and final verses.**

- **Escape by force:** the source-shaped route leaves a blinded host and a crew with losses. “The cave roared out the name we gave the darkness. / Our oars beat home beneath the sound of pain.”
- **Shared flight:** invented route needs a prepared exit and the satyrs' real help; the host remains dangerous to later visitors. “The loudest boasts grew quiet at the shoreline. / We counted friends, then laughed against the waves.”
- **Crew first:** the ship departs while some captives remain; a later rescue or debt verse stays open. “Our sail rose full above the captive hillside. / Their voices travelled farther than our laughter.”

**Carry:** the cup moves from welcome to trap. The satyrs' comic chorus can open Aristophanes's scenes. The sailors who died keep their place in the poem. A promised return becomes a concrete later quest.

## Source review and editorial care

The MIT pages available in this research truncate several texts. The complete CHS texts support the closing scenes for Heracles, Helen, and Hippolytus. The complete Coleridge translation supports Ion's recognition and final public story. The Aulis entry identifies the disputed received ending and treats each outcome as a named telling. The proposed quests and verses are original adaptation writing. All nineteen source plays have been checked for the specific scenes used here.

The main design should keep ordinary people visible: servants at Pherae, the nurse at Corinth, the farmer at Argos, shrine attendants, captives, and families who bear a ruler's choice. Their named work gives the epic its size.
