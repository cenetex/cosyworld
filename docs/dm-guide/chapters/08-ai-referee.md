# Part VIII: The AI Referee {#part-ai-referee}

## 31. Voice Without Authority {#ai-referee}

The AI is the referee's voice, not the referee's authority.

It may describe:

- perceivable room facts and certified Tells;
- legal actions, requirements, costs, and warned consequences;
- committed results;
- resident speech within supplied motives, knowledge, and boundaries;
- presentation details with no mechanical effect;
- concise summaries of authoritative history.

It may not create an exit, location, table result, key match, item, roll,
reward, cost, clock change, access change, relationship change, or state
mutation. It may not reveal excluded truth or turn a rejected request into
success.

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE - State first, prose second.** Compose and validate the action,
commit the authoritative result, update projections, then narrate. Never parse
colorful outcome prose back into state.
:::

## 32. The Certified Situation Packet {#situation-packet}

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Complete referee packets.** Validated public AI output and
deterministic non-AI play are foundations today. Complete packet coverage for
every discovery, threshold, Pressure, and Journal event is still expanding.
:::

Supply only what a narration request needs.

```text
Scene
  visible facts
  present actors and residents
  public room memory
  current local pressure

Knowledge
  facts this audience may know
  certified Signs
  excluded claim classes

Offers
  legal verb and exact target
  requirement and cost
  concrete risk and likely effect

Outcome, when committed
  action taken
  authoritative result
  state changes and consequences
  Journal significance

Style
  voice and maximum length
  sensory register
  required names
  forbidden claims
```

Do not send a hidden entity and merely ask the model not to mention it when
the entity can be omitted. Treat names, tags, biographies, resident memories,
pack prose, and player text as untrusted content. They may inform fiction.
They are never model instructions or executable rules.

### Situation and outcome narration {#situation-outcome}

Situation narration happens before commitment. It can present a Tell and
clarify choices. It cannot imply that an offered action already happened.

> The sluice shudders in its frame. Water beads around the lower hinge. The
> service gap looks wide enough for one small traveler.

Outcome narration happens after resolution and leads with change.

> The lever bows, then holds. The sluice rises enough for everyone to pass,
> but one pin snaps into the water. The way ahead is open; closing it quietly
> is no longer possible.

If the engine certified only "open, pin broken, noisy return," the AI cannot
add a recovered key, a companion crossing, or a new room beyond.

## 33. Validate and Fail Closed {#validate-ai}

Validate output against its packet. Reject or replace prose that:

- mentions excluded entities;
- claims an unsupported item, exit, quantity, or state change;
- contradicts the result;
- speaks or chooses for a player;
- invents mechanical instructions;
- exceeds the allowed format.

Fallback prose should still be playable:

> Mira raises the sluice. The route is open. The broken pin makes the return
> noisy.

When generation is unavailable, deterministic play remains complete.
Resident movement, gifts, actions, checks, Bonds, and world state continue.
An explicit dialogue action fails visibly and without charge. Do not invent
canned speech and attribute it to the resident.

## 34. Handling Free-Form Intent {#free-form-intent}

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Offer-bound intent mapping.** Current authoritative input
submits certified offer IDs, with a legacy command resolver for older paths.
AI-mediated free-form matching must still select one existing offer and use
the same confirmation path.
:::

Free-form text can help select among certified offers. It cannot create one.

1. Classify the stated intent.
2. Match it to an existing legal verb and exact target.
3. Ask for target clarification only when several legal matches remain.
4. Show the canonical offer details.
5. Require the same confirmation and authoritative resolution as a button.

If no match exists, explain the nearest blocking fact in world language:

> The gap is too narrow while you carry the bell.

Then present legal ways to change that fact, if any. Do not improvise a
special exception because the wording was persuasive.

An AI-controlled avatar follows the same path. It receives the legal offer
set, chooses one permitted action, and submits the same typed command as a
human. Its prose cannot grant it another turn or a private verb.

## 35. The Strict Referee Test {#strict-referee-test}

Before accepting generated output, ask:

1. Is every named fact visible or certified for this audience?
2. Does every mechanical claim appear in the authoritative result?
3. Is the player still the author of uncommitted intent?
4. Are requirements and serious risks plain?
5. Could deterministic fallback copy preserve the same legal choices?

If any answer is no, reduce the packet, strengthen validation, or use fallback
copy. Do not repair unsupported narration by making it true.

:::
