# Part I: The World and Its Referee {#part-world-referee}

## 1. The Promise of CosyWorld {#promise}

CosyWorld supports three overlapping fantasies.

- The **Homemaker** cares for a place and makes it more welcoming.
- The **Helper** answers needs and becomes someone others can rely on.
- The **Wanderer** follows uncertain signs and brings discoveries home.

None is secondary. Combat can matter, but the game is not organized around
combat. Cards can be collected, but ownership does not replace adventure.
Residents can speak through AI, but the game is not a collection of private
chatbots. The game is one persistent world and the changes people make
together.

A player should be able to say three things after a short first visit:

- "This is who I am."
- "This is where I belong."
- "This is what I am trying to change."

Home gives the journey meaning. The frontier gives the return meaning. The
Journal connects both by recording what became true.

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE - Play is not for sale.** Ordinary actions, relationships, travel,
discovery, and growth never require Orbs. Orbs may pay for shared image
generation. They never buy success, access, turns, power, or advancement.
Core play continues without generative AI.
:::

## 2. The Strict, Generous Referee {#strict-generous-referee}

Use this procedure whenever the situation changes.

1. **Establish the situation.** Name the place, present actors, and immediate
   question.
2. **State the obvious.** Give the physical facts a person there can readily
   know.
3. **Present legal approaches.** Show the exact two-card action hand and its
   certified Think/Pass; keep the complete legal candidate set internal.
4. **Clarify commitment.** Name the target, cost, visible risk, and likely
   scale of effect.
5. **Resolve once.** Let the authoritative rules produce the result.
6. **Apply the whole consequence.** Update state, then describe only what the
   result supports.
7. **Record what mattered.** Public changes enter shared history.

Do not ask for a check merely because a task sounds difficult. Difficulty
matters only when present conditions create uncertainty and a meaningful
consequence.

- An exact key used on its matching lock works.
- A clear inscription read in good light is understood.
- A familiar bridge crossed in calm weather needs no test.

Conversely, do not hide a meaningful cost behind a harmless label. If forcing
a gate may draw a patrol, say so before offering **Force**. If resting may
advance danger, say so before **Rest**. If a statue is too heavy to carry, show
the requirement before **Take**.

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - No null commitments.** A committed turn changes position,
knowledge, resources, pressure, opportunity, condition, or relationship.
Failure can worsen matters, reveal a hard truth, consume an opportunity, or
force a new approach. It must not mean "nothing happens; try again."
:::

### Example: the rusted garden gate {#example-garden-gate}

The referee might certify these approaches:

| Approach            | Requirement            | Commitment                       |
| ------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Use the brass key   | Carry the matching key | Safe, certain, quiet             |
| Lift the hinge pins | Carry the proper tool  | Safe, certain, slow              |
| Force the gate      | Enough leverage        | Risk noise or injury             |
| Search the wall     | None                   | Spend time to seek another route |
| Leave               | None                   | Preserve the present situation   |

The referee does not choose for the player. It makes the choice honest.

## 3. Who Decides What {#authority}

CosyWorld separates authority so expressive narration never becomes an
accidental rules engine.

![Authored possibilities become legal actions, a player commits, the engine resolves, the Journal records, and the referee describes the certified result](assets/diagrams/authority-loop.svg)

| Layer        | May decide                                                                                    | Must not decide                                                   |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Worldpack    | Places, residents, items, routes, tables, gates, and bounded possibilities                    | Live outcomes or state outside its schema                         |
| Server       | Scene composition, legal actions, costs, access, persistence, public projection, and ordering | Outcomes reserved for the rules engine                            |
| Rules engine | Legality, checks, movement, possession, harm, and authoritative mutations                     | Prose, mood, or unvalidated content                               |
| AI           | Public narration, resident speech, summaries, and presentation proposals                      | Facts, access, rewards, rolls, costs, inventory, or state changes |
| Client       | Display, accessibility, and submission of a chosen legal action                               | Legality, affordability, success, or hidden truth                 |
| Player       | Intent, target, approach, speech, and acceptance of a known risk                              | Unilateral changes to shared state                                |

A worldpack defines what might exist. The server composes the complete legal
candidate set from the present scene, then deals its authoritative two-card
hand and certified Think/Pass. The player commits one of those actions. The
rules engine resolves it. The server persists and broadcasts the resulting
events.
AI can then turn those certified facts into fitting public prose.

Rewards and one-use changes need stable claim keys. Retrying a request,
reconnecting, or replaying the event history must never grant the same thing
twice.

When AI is unavailable, deterministic actions still resolve. Incidental
resident speech may be skipped. An explicit dialogue action fails openly and
without charge. The system does not impersonate unavailable intelligence with
invented fallback speech.

Keep three kinds of statement distinct:

- A **fact** is committed state the engine can prove.
- An **offer** is an action the server currently certifies as legal.
- **Color** is sensory or emotional presentation that changes no rule.

Color may enrich a fact. It cannot create one.

## 4. The Laws of Play {#laws-of-play}

### One world means one history {#one-history}

The official service has one canonical world. Players in the same room meet
the same room, residents, items, and public events. Self-hosted installations
are separate worlds, not private copies of the official one.

### Home is safe by rule {#home-safe}

A sanctuary rejects combat and autonomous danger. It does not decay while
nobody is playing. A dim cottage may be mysterious and a resident may be
worried, but neither permits involuntary harm at Home.

### Stakes require consent {#stakes-consent}

Danger begins when a player knowingly enters a risky place, accepts dangerous
work, or commits to an action with stated consequences. Consent to one danger
is not permission for unrelated harm elsewhere.

### The world runs on played time {#played-time}

World time advances through committed actions, not merely because a wall clock
moved. Weather, trade pressure, faction movement, and conflict may advance
from bounded played-time pulses. They cannot silently punish an absent player.

### No danger, no roll {#no-danger-no-roll}

If an action is legal, safe, and certain, it succeeds. A roll answers one
uncertain question with an actual consequence. It does not decide whether an
already-authored fact exists.

### Warn before commitment {#warn-before-commitment}

Show requirements, costs, visible risks, and expected scale before
confirmation. Hidden truth may remain hidden; visible danger should not.

### A committed turn changes something {#turn-changes-something}

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE - No null discovery or method retries.** Versioned discovery
freezes and advances one claim, while concrete threshold methods either apply
their effect and consequence or become unavailable after failure. Repeating
the same request cannot reroll fixed truth or restore an exhausted approach.
:::

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Universal no null commitments.** Every remaining committed
procedure should apply the same law: change a meaningful fact or reject before
spending the turn.
:::

Success and failure both move the situation. Repeating an approach does not
reroll hidden truth. If the opportunity was consumed, say so. If another
approach remains legal, show it.

### Recovery remains reachable {#recovery-reachable}

Danger may force retreat, loss, exhaustion, or rescue. It must not remove
every route back into play. Required keys need recovery rules. A failed
expedition may close an opportunity without trapping an avatar forever. If the
Fatigue playtest is active, Spent preserves its defined survival hand.

**ACCEPTED TARGET - Recovery proof.** World validation should prove every
required discovery, key, Gate, Hazard, and exhaustion state retains a legal
route forward or Home; complete automated proof remains in progress.

### Progression is earned {#progression-earned}

Growth follows meaningful play: living a Calling, changing a relationship,
learning a truth, helping, or returning from risk. It is not purchased.

### Public actions have public consequences {#public-consequences}

Resident speech, movement, checks, discoveries, gifts, and world changes
appear as room events to everyone present. Account details may be private. The
shared world does not run secret parallel scenes.

### AI performs; rules decide {#ai-performs}

Generated prose can make the world vivid. It cannot determine truth. If prose
conflicts with authoritative state, state wins and the prose is corrected or
omitted.
:::
