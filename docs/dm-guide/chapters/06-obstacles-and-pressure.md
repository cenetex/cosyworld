# Part VI: Obstacles and Pressure {#part-obstacles}

![A cellar threshold shows a plain keyway, disturbed dust, a bell cord, and light from the far side](assets/illustrations/readable-threshold.jpg)

## 23. The Obstacle Grammar {#obstacle-grammar}

A CosyWorld obstacle is assembled from four small parts.

| Part     | Question                                               |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lead     | What can the players follow, investigate, or act upon? |
| Gate     | What permits access or change?                         |
| Hazard   | What is dangerous, and how is it signaled?             |
| Pressure | Why does delay, repetition, or position matter?        |

Use only what the situation needs. A locked cottage door may be only a Gate.
A trapped coffer is a Gate plus Hazard. A fading track is a Lead plus
Pressure. A flooded ruin may use all four.

These parts remain separate because topology, legibility, access, and safety
are different facts. A route can exist without being known, be known without
being passable, be passable while dangerous, or be safe while a distant storm
still creates pressure.

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE - Typed descriptors and concrete Open.** Versioned Lead, Gate,
Hazard, Pressure, and Anchor descriptors are live. The current `open_v1`
transition freezes one authored method's Gate evidence, requirement, cost,
resolution policy, effect, consequence, and disabled reason. Exact keys resolve
certainly and quietly. Safe tool work resolves certainly for its stated time.
Dangerous work consumes its turn and, after failure, disables that identical
method. Open never spends Orbs, reveals hidden contents, or transfers them.
:::

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Unified obstacle coverage.** The descriptors are the shared
substrate for trails, doors, containers, traps, chases, and dungeons. Full
Hazard, Pressure, discovery, and authoring coverage remains in progress.
:::

### Leads {#leads}

A Lead is a presently actionable connection between known evidence and
something that may be discovered.

Good Leads are concrete: hoofprints across a wet lane, soot breathing through
a crack, bells remembered beneath a hill, torn cord in alder roots, or a line
on a shared map. "The woods feel mysterious" is mood, not a Lead.

Record source, target or target class, who knows it, legibility, the action
that advances it, and what can preserve, weaken, or close it. A Lead need not
identify its destination. It must identify a meaningful next action.

### Gates {#gates}

A Gate is a condition between an actor and an effect. Doors are common Gates,
but permission, tools, standing, knowledge, installed relics, one-use seals,
and holder qualifications are Gates too.

Write one as: **An actor may make this transition if this requirement is true,
using one of these methods.**

If a requirement is perceivable, show it before commitment. Otherwise provide
a Sign and a legal way to learn it.

### Hazards {#hazards}

Every Hazard has:

- a **Tell** perceived before commitment;
- a **Trigger** tied to an action or state;
- a bounded **Consequence**;
- a current **State**, such as armed, disarmed, triggered, spent, or reset.

A Hazard without a Tell is punishment, not choice. "The third flagstone sits
low, and dust avoids its edges" is enough warning. Focused Notice, Search, or
Study may reveal more.

### Pressure {#pressure}

Pressure explains why players cannot examine everything forever: a patrol,
rain erasing a track, floodwater, a frightened animal, settling masonry,
distance in a chase, or finite supply.

Pressure advances through committed relevant play. It does not advance because
a player closed the game or paused to read. Use a clock only when the pressure
has several meaningful stages.

## 24. Doors, Keys, and Containers {#doors-keys-containers}

A threshold is interesting when it creates a choice. If the only response is
"find the one key somewhere else," it is an errand marker.

Prepare an important threshold with this record:

| Field    | Question                                        |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| State    | Open, closed, locked, sealed, broken, or spent? |
| Tell     | What is obvious before interaction?             |
| Scope    | Who benefits when it changes?                   |
| Methods  | Which exact approaches work?                    |
| Cost     | What does each method consume or risk?          |
| Change   | Open, bypass, damage, install, or transform?    |
| Return   | How can someone on the far side come back?      |
| Recovery | What if a required item becomes unavailable?    |

Do not write only "DC 14 door." Write the thing in the world:

> The alder sluice is swollen shut. Water pulses through its lower seam. The
> iron key opens it quietly. A fitted lever raises it but risks bending the
> pins. Draining the mill race opens it safely after two turns. A small actor
> can crawl through alone while the grate remains shut.

### Key forms {#key-forms}

Keep different access contracts explicit.

| Form               | Rule                                                       |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Portable exact key | A carried reusable item answers one named lock             |
| Capability         | A tool or trait answers an authored class of problems      |
| Installed key      | An item works while placed in a socket, plinth, or machine |
| Consumable seal    | One valid use changes the Gate and spends the item         |
| Charged item       | It works while authoritative charges remain                |
| Holder permission  | Only qualifying holders pass                               |
| Shared unlock      | The threshold changes for everyone                         |
| Known phrase       | Repeatable lore checked by the engine                      |
| Social permission  | A Bond, Job, resident, or room fact grants access          |

An exact valid key in a safe accessible lock works without a check.
Capabilities operate only where an authored method says they do.

Never treat a remembered phrase as private ownership. A phrase can be a
repeatable public condition. Exclusive permission must remain attached to
authoritative identity, state, or custody.

Give a consequential Gate at least two useful methods unless singularity is
the point of the adventure. Methods should differ in kind:

| Method         | Certain effect          | Possible pressure cost                |
| -------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Exact key      | Open quietly            | None                                  |
| Lock tools     | Open without damage     | Time, detection, or tool wear         |
| Force          | Open or break           | Noise, harm, evidence, or lost return |
| Remote release | Open from elsewhere     | Travel and discovery                  |
| Permission     | Open lawfully           | Promise, Bond, or completed work      |
| Bypass         | Reach the far side only | Separation or difficult retreat       |

The roll belongs to pressure, not to basic key function. If a patrol is three
turns away and lock tools take two turns, the action can certainly open the
lock while a check decides whether the work is noticed.

### Access scope and return {#access-scope}

State whether the result applies to one actor, named actors, the expedition,
or the world. Do not carry unqualified actors through a holder-only Gate.
Removing the item later affects future passage, not past position.

A Gate that may close behind the party needs at least one return contract:
inside release, available key, occupancy interlock, alternate route, authored
rescue, or an explicit consented sealing risk with recovery. "The AI will
invent a way out" is not a contract.

For every unique key, answer where it begins, how it can move or be destroyed,
what happens if its holder is absent or its pack is removed, what alternate
method remains, and when the Gate can become irrelevant. A key able to leave
the world must not guard its only required path.

### Containers {#containers}

A container asks four independent questions:

1. Can it be found?
2. Can it be opened?
3. Can its contents be revealed?
4. Can revealed contents be taken?

Record placement, visible description, hidden state, Gate and Hazard,
fixed contents source, allocation scope, capacity, empty presentation, and
mobility. Establish contents once. Closing and reopening never restocks them.

A one-time seal or world-once cache uses an authoritative claim. The first
valid commitment records actor, object, place, reason, and state change.
Concurrent attempts observe the new state or fail cleanly without duplication.

## 25. Items, Zones, and Capacity {#items-capacity}

Avatars, items, and locations are the three collectible subjects. Weapons,
skills, spells, keys, bags, and relics are item roles, not separate ownership
systems. Card text may contribute an action, modifier, capability, or target.
It never executes a rule.

| Zone               | Meaning                                                      |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Collection         | Account-owned cards, including those not in this world       |
| Carried deck       | Physical item instances the avatar carries                   |
| Equipped           | Items active in typed slots                                  |
| Spell deck or hand | Prepared and currently usable spell items                    |
| Exhausted          | Items unavailable until allowed recovery                     |
| World              | Physical item instances placed at a location, with no holder |
| Contained          | Items inside one valid container                             |
| Escrow             | Items held during atomic transfer                            |
| Action hand        | Temporary legal offers, never possessions                    |

Every transfer names instance, source, destination, actor, reason, and claim.
Seeing a card does not grant it. Retrying a transfer does not duplicate it.

Capacity is physical. It comes from the avatar's body and size, item weight and
bulk, valid equipped containers, container openings, and typed slots. A bag
inside another bag does not contribute capacity. Each physical item occupies
one placement zone; Collection is the account ownership record, and the action
hand is only a projection.

If capacity blocks Take, name the conflict and offer legal responses: Set
Down, Give, rearrange into a valid container, return with equipment, or leave
the item marked in the world. Never delete or replace excess items.

An important item declares role, weight and bulk, slots, supported actions and
targets, uses or durability, recovery, transfer policy, and provenance.

## 26. Hazards, Chases, and Conflict {#hazards-chases-conflict}

### Hazard procedure {#hazard-procedure}

1. Restate the perceivable Tell.
2. Ask for intent, method, and target.
3. State the method's certain effect.
4. Warn of the important risk.
5. Commit time and resources.
6. Resolve only the remaining uncertainty.
7. Apply the consequence and new Hazard state.
8. Record the public result.

Methods contact different triggers. If a bell cord crosses a trapped window,
cutting it may drop the bell, untying it may disturb the latch, bracing the
weight may make untying safe, pulling from outside may sound the alarm, and
breaking the frame may make loud permanent access.

A failed action cannot leave an armed Hazard and every other fact unchanged.
It normally triggers, advances pressure, damages the method, changes position,
or exhausts the approach.

### Pressure scenes and chases {#pressure-scenes}

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Unified Pressure scenes.** The current game has checks,
clocks, ordered combat, Defend, Flee, and nonlethal defeat. The paired-clock
chase procedure below is the accepted extension, not a universal shipped
resolver.
:::

Use paired clocks when both achievement and approaching trouble need several
beats.

```text
Progress: Open the floodgate      [ ][ ][ ][ ]
Danger: Water fills the chamber  [ ][ ][ ][ ]
```

Each action may advance progress, improve position, redirect danger, protect
someone, create a method, or withdraw. Failure usually advances danger;
partial success may advance both. Filling a clock changes the world.

Use a chase only when both sides can change the outcome through several
decisions. Otherwise make one check. A standard chase uses an objective clock
and a danger clock. Each round:

1. describe distance, terrain, and the next obstacle;
2. offer fast, secure, tool, Help, and withdrawal approaches as relevant;
3. resolve participants under the scene's authored concurrency policy, using
   ordered turns only when that policy is scene-turn;
4. resolve progress and danger;
5. change the terrain or choice;
6. end when a clock fills or someone withdraws.

Avoid identical running checks. Each beat should pose a different problem.

### Conflict has an objective {#conflict-objectives}

Name the objective before opponents: save lambs from smoke, hold the bridge,
recover the bell, or escape with the map. Attack, Defend, Use, Help, and Flee
are methods. Defeat changes the situation rather than killing by default; Flee
succeeds when it protects the chosen objective.
:::
