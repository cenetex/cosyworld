# Welcome {#welcome}

CosyWorld is a shared adventure game about having somewhere safe to belong
and choosing when to leave it.

This book is for human referees, worldpack authors, and the people building
CosyWorld's strict AI referee. It explains how to make a world feel generous
without letting improvisation change the rules. It is also an honest map of a
game in motion: some procedures are live, some are accepted foundations still
being built, and a few are deliberate playtests.

::: {.edition-note}
**First working edition - 30 July 2026.** The rules-status labels below are

## How to read the rule labels {#rule-labels}

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE**

This is current behavior or a foundation the live game already relies on.
Worldpacks and interfaces should preserve it.
:::

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET**

The direction is decided, but part or all of the procedure is not shipped.
Use it to author forward-compatible content. Do not promise the missing
interaction to players yet.
:::

::: {.playtest}
**PLAYTEST**

This is an experiment, not product law. Measure it in play before promoting
it to a core rule.
:::

::: {.referee}
**REFEREE PRACTICE**

This is advice for presenting and adjudicating the rules. It cannot override
authoritative state.
:::

## The whole game in one page {#whole-game}

![The CosyWorld loop moves from hearth to a need or sign, through a chosen venture and discovery, then home with change](assets/diagrams/core-loop.svg)

At Home, players understand who they are and what matters. A need, promise, or
sign invites action. The players prepare, then choose whether to cross into
uncertainty. In the frontier they discover, help, negotiate, build, flee, or
fight. What they secure is carried home. The world remembers the result, and
the next venture begins from a changed place.

The referee protects that loop with four questions:

1. What can the players understand now?
2. Which meaningful actions are legal now?
3. What might change if they commit?
4. How will the world remember the result?

A good turn answers all four. A good visit creates at least one reason to
return.

## The first principle {#first-principle}

The governing principle is **strict truth and generous information**. Part I
turns that principle into a procedure.
