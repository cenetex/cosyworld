# Part II: People, Home, and Memory {#part-people-home}

## 5. Avatars, Callings, and Bonds {#avatars-callings-bonds}

An avatar is a person in the world, not a cursor and not a private AI
companion.

The staged creation flow begins with identity cards. A new player chooses a
Species and Origin, then enters at level 0. Their first positive contribution
to an authored Job freezes the evidence that made the contribution meaningful
and reveals every available Class. The campaign may recommend a Class that
matches the contributed approach, but every Class remains selectable. Choosing
a Class completes the starting composition, raises the avatar to level 1,
provides a first approach and kit, and supplies a Calling.

```text
SPECIES + ORIGIN -> POSITIVE JOB CONTRIBUTION -> CLASS + CALLING
```

Species describes body, scale, senses, and visual language. Origin establishes
a truthful connection to a place or tradition. Class describes learned work
and a starting approach. Long-term identity comes from carried items, equipped
charms, relationships, public history, and later choices rather than a fixed
class tree.

A **Calling** is a short statement about who the avatar is trying to be:

- "I repair what others have abandoned."
- "I carry stories safely home."
- "I leave every shelter warmer than I found it."

A Calling grants no automatic power. It gives public action personal meaning.
It may be revised at a later growth milestone to express change through play.

A **Bond** is a relationship stated as a concrete truth:

- "Mira trusts me with the greenhouse keys."
- "I owe Rowan an honest answer."
- "The Rain-Soft Garden is where I go to think."

A Bond can form, deepen, strain, or resolve. Resolution does not erase it. The
Journal records that its statement no longer describes the present
relationship.

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Broader Bonds.** Resident Bonds are live. Bonds to places,
other players, factions, and Covenants are not yet equally supported.
:::

::: {.referee}
**REFEREE PRACTICE - Invite identity.** Give the repairer something damaged,
the collector an overlooked account, and the promise-keeper a choice that
tests the promise. Do not turn a Calling into a command. Change a Bond only
when the relationship changes, not after repeated button presses.
:::

## 6. Home and Sanctuary {#home-sanctuary}

Home is the fixed point of a CosyWorld tale. Every avatar begins with access
to a sanctuary, such as a cottage or another authored starting home. It is
part of the canonical world and personally meaningful.

Sanctuary obeys three absolute rules:

1. Combat is not legal there.
2. Autonomous danger and decay do not advance there.
3. Frontier pressure cannot advance sanctuary danger or impose involuntary
   loss there. Players may choose to leave or undertake opt-in local work.

Safety does not mean inactivity. Home can hold relationships, useful work,
discoveries, unfinished promises, and long projects. These invite
participation instead of threatening loss through neglect.

Run Home as the richest place to understand, not the menu between
expeditions. On arrival, present a local action besides Travel. Let residents
react to what returned. Let carried objects connect to room features. Show
changes caused by earlier work. A player who never seeks danger should still
have a complete loop of care, friendship, discovery, and growth.

The return is a mechanical threshold. Ask:

- What did the avatar bring back?
- Who notices?
- Which promise changed?
- What can be done here now that was impossible before?

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE - Hearth recovery.** A sanctuary supports the strongest Rest
grade. It clears tired, training strain, and expedition depth, refreshes the
full spell hand, and refreshes eligible charms and relics.
:::

Do not smuggle frontier pressure into Home to manufacture urgency. A resident
may report trouble outside and a Job may remain open. The player still chooses
whether to leave.

## 7. Residents and Shared Rooms {#residents-shared-rooms}

Residents inhabit the public world. Each needs an authored home, persona,
wants, boundaries, memory references, reaction state, and allowed actions.
AI may phrase a response, but it cannot grant a new power, move an unavailable
item, ignore a boundary, or change a relationship by narration.

A provider model is not enough to make a resident. If an endpoint only creates
images, video, speech audio, transcripts, semantic matches, rankings, or music,
represent it as a curious item device unless the worldpack separately authors a
genuine agent. A device may be carried, equipped, or installed; the avatar using
it remains the actor. This follows ADR 0007, *Model Bindings and Item Devices*.

A useful resident answers four questions:

1. What do they want now?
2. What can they legally do about it?
3. What will they refuse?
4. What visible sign reveals their present intention?

Residents may move, remember committed facts, and pursue desired items through
deterministic behavior. Their actions occur on played time and become public
events. A resident seeking a lost keepsake may travel toward its known
location. They do not teleport to private information.

Ordinary co-present play is concurrent. Players need not wait in a universal
room queue. Actions against the same contested target are serialized by the
server, and stale attempts are rejected or recomposed. Only an explicitly
ordered scene, such as combat, uses scene-turn order. Speech and emotes remain
turn-exempt, subject to ordinary presence and moderation checks.

::: {.rule}
**CORE RULE - Controller parity.** Human-controlled and inference-controlled
avatars use the same legal actions. The controller chooses; it does not gain
different possession, costs, targets, checks, Bonds, or access rules.
:::

A contextual action may invite one bounded resident response. Rapid actions
should not stack a chorus of replies. Authored priority decides who responds
first.

## 8. The Journal and Growth {#journal-growth}

CosyWorld keeps two related records.

The **canonical journal** is the authoritative event history. It preserves
exact actions, outcomes, claims, and replay order. Players do not edit it, and
AI does not summarize facts into it.

The player-facing **Journal** is a readable projection of that history. Its
target shape has three regions:

- **Current place:** what is true and actionable here.
- **Open threads:** promises, needs, Leads, Jobs, and unresolved changes.
- **Story so far:** concise, causal memories of meaningful play.

"Elsie discovered a path to the Old Oak Tree" is useful history. Separate
rows for a search event, a route tag, a path mutation, and a movement flag
are not.

The Visit Ledger can mark that an avatar:

- followed their Calling;
- deepened or resolved a Bond;
- learned a true thing;
- helped another actor or shared effort;
- dared the frontier and returned.

Marks are earned once for meaningful causes. Stable claim keys stop retries,
reconnects, and replay from granting the same mark twice. Eligible marks
settle into advancement. Growth is then spent on an expressive choice, such
as making room for a carried skill charm, adding or revising a Bond slot, or
revising a Calling when eligible. Opening a bracelet slot does not create or
equip the charm; the item must still be found, carried, and chosen.

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Semantic Journal.** The authoritative event history is
live. The clean current place, open threads, and story so far projection is
groomed but not yet complete across every event type.
:::

## 9. Jobs, Fronts, and Covenants {#jobs-fronts-covenants}

These are authoring terms. Player-facing copy should usually say that someone
needs help, trouble is gathering, or Home has a project.

### Jobs {#jobs}

A **Job** is a bounded need with a premise, stakes, relevant participants and
places, more than one progress method, danger, a reward or durable change, and
a consequence if matters worsen.

A Job is not a checklist delivered by a quest dispenser. If a greenhouse is
losing warmth, players might repair a vent, bring fuel, study the frost,
persuade a resident to share a device, or find the source of the cold. Every
method must bind to a legal authored action.

Good Jobs change something visible: a room feature, resident behavior, route,
item custody, service, or new opportunity.

### Fronts {#fronts}

A **Front** is a larger frontier pressure or opportunity that creates several
situations over played time. It has a premise, cast, open stakes questions,
and an approaching outcome. It does not prescribe a plot.

- Will the flooded road be restored or abandoned?
- Who will control the salvage route?
- Can the wandering lights be understood before the path closes?

Player action answers those questions. Front pressure remains local to the
frontier and advances only from committed play. It cannot decay Home or
punish absent players.

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Job and Front breadth.** Seed Job state, the first complete
Job resolution, and early Front records are live. Multi-method bindings,
completion memories and rewards, player-turn Front progression, and
Front-spawned Jobs remain incomplete.
:::

### Covenants {#covenants}

A **Covenant** is the accepted shared-Home direction: a named cottage, guild,
school, or similar community shaped together through boons, resources,
projects, reputation, and personal loyalty.

The shared result and individual contribution both remain visible. A restored
garden belongs to the Covenant; the Journal still remembers who found the
seed, repaired the wall, and kept watch.

::: {.target}
**ACCEPTED TARGET - Covenant play.** Sanctuary rooms, Bonds, room memory,
Jobs, and early Front records exist. Full Covenant membership, resources,
projects, loyalty, and generated work are future phases. Authors may
foreshadow them but must not expose unavailable verbs.
:::

Use these structures only when persistent state and later choices need them.
Not every kindness needs a Job. Not every storm needs a Front. Not every
shared room needs a Covenant.
:::
