# A world that remembers

> CosyWorld is a world that remembers.
>
> Its inhabitants may be mistaken. They may disagree, deceive, desire things
> they cannot have. They inherit strange fragments of the stories that made
> them.
>
> But the world itself does not lie.
>
> An object must be somewhere. A promise must have been made. A door opens for
> a reason. A kindness leaves a trace.
>
> Intelligence gives the world its voices. Play gives those voices consequence.
>
> We do not measure a world by how much it contains, but by how much can
> matter.

This is the ethos beside [the Pact](cosyworld-pact.md). The Pact is product
law: what the game promises every traveler. This page is editorial: how we
decide what belongs in the game. It is a principle for choosing, not a
framework every feature must satisfy.

## Four things the game needs

AI can produce almost limitless imagination. It cannot reliably judge whether
the result is worth experiencing. More generation is not more game.

- **The model's imagination** supplies voices and candidates.
- **The rules' integrity** keeps custody, consent, access and outcomes true.
- **The author's taste** keeps the few candidates worth keeping and cuts the
  rest.
- **The player's freedom** to help, refuse, retreat or return gives what
  remains a history.

The Pact sections this leans on most are One World, One Truth; Discovery
Begins With Evidence; Return Makes The Difference Public; and AI Has A Voice,
Not Authority.

## Three questions for a gameplay issue

Before a gameplay feature enters the active backlog, answer in plain language:

1. What can the player actually do differently?
2. What will the world remember afterward?
3. Why is this more interesting than what already exists?

An issue with no good answer may still be worthwhile infrastructure. Admit it
as infrastructure; do not present it as a new gameplay experience.

A gameplay issue also carries one observable acceptance test, a clear
dependency, and a player-facing reason to exist.

## What we cut

Deprioritize generic generated quest chains, rooms without a new purpose,
repetitive dialogue, automatic quality judges, and mechanics that exist only
to justify another system.

Grooming should reduce the amount of work, not only organize it. Close or
consolidate an issue whose premise the current game has overtaken, after
checking current code and its open PRs, and link the surviving owner.

## Editorial evidence

There is no AI judge, score, or certification process for experience. One
human plays an unfamiliar level, names the most memorable moment, then names
the part they would remove. That is enough to guide the next iteration.
[Designing past the jagged edge](design/jagged-agi-level-design.md) gives the
longer review prompts and the journeys worth trying.

## Reference levels

- **RatiRadio** differentiates consecutive rooms without adding mechanics. Its
  return restores a relationship, not another locked area
  ([#2577](https://github.com/cenetex/cosyworld/issues/2577)).
- **The Wine-Dark Sea** shows moral consequence. A decree, testimony, burial
  or refusal can change the public world; its force comes from conflicting
  claims, not the length of a chain
  ([#2578](https://github.com/cenetex/cosyworld/issues/2578)).
- **The Lonely Forest** shows intimate discovery. A lamp, a scarf, an oak and
  a familiar path change meaning through use; its strangeness stays attached
  to concrete things
  ([#2579](https://github.com/cenetex/cosyworld/issues/2579)).
- **The world builder** shows editorial freedom. AI summons candidates, the
  author chooses, the player gives those creations a history.

Active work under this ethos is indexed in
[#2576](https://github.com/cenetex/cosyworld/issues/2576).

The machine can summon a thousand forests. CosyWorld should make one forest
worth returning to.
