# A dynamic world for the 44 Greek plays

Give each play a distinct problem at its authored location. The player hears a claim or request, acts with the people and objects at hand, and leaves a consequence that changes what someone can do. An agreement can open a stall. A shelter pledge can give someone a place to stay. A witnessed account can give a later hearing evidence to consider. A consequence can reach another district when an authorized person hears it or receives a record of it.

The world uses one set of rules for these scenes. The 44 plays supply the places, people, disputes and compatible accounts. The map supplies the cost and opportunity of moving between them. The existing card actions, commitments, custody and journal supply the state. The catalog remains the record of which plays and scenes make up the repertoire. ## From a local choice to a lasting consequence

1. **Learn the situation.** A named person, an object or a visible change introduces a problem at a particular place. A traveler learns who can act, what they need, and what is at stake through the available cards and journal.
2. **Act on it.** Take or read evidence, Talk to a witness, give an object to someone who has authority over it, or accompany someone who has agreed to travel. The venue, evidence, recipient and account determine which actions are available. A player can leave and pick up the scene later.
3. **Commit a material result.** The accepted play records the outcome and applies its effects together. Where relevant, the rules change custody, open a local route, admit a witness, grant refuge, register a hearing or change the goods a person offers. A return visit should reveal at least one such result.
4. **Carry the result forward.** A returning witness, a public notice read at its site, or a record carried to a named recipient can give a second scene new terms. A traveler who missed the event can learn a sourced account without receiving credit for witnessing it.

Thirty-five encounters start with Talk. Thirty-one then offer two alternative Give actions, and four offer a choice on a resident’s card. Each outcome records a receipt and makes a specific consequence available. The public and private outcomes in _Acharnians_ also change what routes and goods the player can use. ### Selecting a scene

Author a scene with a stable work and choice ID, venue, known participants, trigger, prerequisites, two or more meaningful responses where appropriate, a concrete effect and a way to learn or recover the result. Use existing predicates such as a choice already made, a flag, a known topic, an item in hand or at a place, and the presence of a required person. Bind each named lead to the play. A supporting role such as a witness or carrier can vary within an authored set of people who are present, eligible and able to do the work.

Show an available next step in a commitment the traveler has already accepted before offering new optional business in the same room. Then offer a relevant local consequence before repeatable talk. Break ties with stable authored order. In the first release, allow one secondary scene per committed result and one authored path to revise its terms. Use a stable follow-up choice ID and its receipt to grant the outcome once in a world, even when another traveler arrives with the same proof. A request can be deferred until the actor or evidence is available. Once the player has accepted a main request, it survives ordinary travel, rest and absence.

For irreversible outcomes in this design, require an eligible human action. Residents can keep routines, carry an agreed object, wait, tell what they know and relocate through existing travel. Give consequential acts an explicit authority check, since resident Give actions also use the existing reducer. Reserve story proof and cargo for their authorized handoff while a request is open, including when an absent avatar is acting as a resident. Use story scope, availability and knowledge to derive card visibility. A closed route can give a neutral clue before discovery and explain its requirements once the traveler has enough evidence. ## Where the system runs

- **Cities have different local authorities.** The Argive jury, an altar keeper and a harbor steward can respond differently to the same type of proof. Store the terms that person can offer, such as a sheltered place or a smaller supply order. Keep trust tied to the people who have reason to change it. A claimed office, a public notice and a privately held letter each give different grounds for action.
- **Era and account are explicit.** Oedipus’s forecourt, the Seventh Gate during the siege and Antigone’s burial aftermath sit in the same Theban travel district. They carry their own cast and prerequisites. A consequence crosses an era or authorial account only through an authored connection, such as a later court admitting a specific earlier record. The competing Atreid accounts and Helen’s Egyptian and Trojan settings retain their established gates. Euripides’ _Orestes_ remains available at the Argive Jury Yard under its current rules.
- **Travel gives information a cost and a source.** Charybdis serves Black Pond, Wine-Dark Harbor, Piraeus and the Signal Jetty. Walk inland through Thebes, Eleusis, Athens, Delphi, Trachis and Pherae to reach Scylla at Pagasae. Scylla reaches Egypt from Pagasae when Helen’s Egyptian account opens and Homecoming Beach from Lemnos when her Trojan account opens. Ajax’s landing is a separate Scylla berth after Myrto’s invitation. The Royal Road continues from Troy to Susa and the Scythian Coast Road continues inland from Tauris. Use actual cards, ship states and learned routes for every leg. [Pack travel notes](../../v2/content/greek-drama/ATTRIBUTION.md#L47) · [Ship and lane definitions](../../v2/worlds/greek-drama/card-bridge.json#L287)
- **Journeys stay legible and resumable.** Show the current berth, next known leg and any discovered requirement. The Harbor-to-Pagasae walk takes 15 Move cards once the way is open. Allow time for learning lanes, waiting for a ship and arranging cargo when a crossing requires it. Advance time through committed turns. An optional market opportunity can depend on a local window if it has a later recovery step. Keep a person with a standing request reachable by a return visit, a fixed record or an authored alternate contact. Protect core stories and safe ways home from unrelated settlements. ## Implementation sequence

1. **Prove the authoring model at Lemnos.** Express _Philoctetes_ in one small, validated scene definition using named roles, venue, evidence, triggers and effects. Generate the native choices, topics and object links. Compare the generated records and both outcomes to the current fixture. Preserve stable IDs and saved semantics while changing how the rules are authored.
2. **Ship the first consequence that travels.** Add the Acharnae and Piraeus interaction using existing choice, fact and custody checks. Test both paths and a returning player who has yet to learn the agreement. Add a new typed condition only when the prototype needs a proof check the current predicates cannot express.
3. **Prove connected plays and long journeys.** Use the Argive household and Areopagus to test compatible accounts and the source of a claim. Exercise _Ajax_ in an actual shared game with a second carrier, a save while aboard Scylla and a return to Dione. The atlas and hand should agree about visible opportunities after every leg.
4. **Apply the patterns to the full repertoire.** Move the repeated episodes across in groups. Keep substantial authored quests such as _Antigone_, _Ajax_ and the Open Door on their existing primitives where those already express the scene. Give each play a visible outcome at its own location and add cross-play consequences where the record has a credible recipient.

The proof of concept can use the current saved shape. Authoring metadata can compile to choices, topics, claims, wants and routes. Index any added lookup by triggering action and venue so ordinary turns only inspect local candidates. Validate overlapping choice triggers, unreachable proof, exclusive account and cast collisions, and safe return routes. Keep published replay bundles pinned. Any later saved field or changed meaning requires a versioned load path with defaults or a targeted migration for older saves. | Acceptance check                             | Passing result                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The same facts reproduce the same result** | The same starting state and actions agree in local and shared play. Turning off generated dialogue yields the same receipts, places, custody and available actions.                             |
| **A second traveler can help**               | The newcomer gains the later opportunity by witnessing, reading or receiving proof. They can tell the source. They cannot claim direct experience of an event they missed.                      |
| **A public result changes play**             | Each pilot outcome changes at least one playable option, access rule or item beyond changing a caption. Both paths have a useful consequence.                                                   |
| **Time away is safe**                        | A save while sailing or while holding evidence returns the player to the same task and position. An unavailable witness has a readable record, a return visit or an authored alternate contact. |
| **A shared action resolves once**            | Simultaneous submissions for the same resource or exclusive outcome yield one accepted result. Subsequent requests see the current result and do not duplicate items, rewards or receipts.      |
