# Earth Beside the Ships

This chapter follows the completed Open Door stories and a return to Dione.
Myrto offers a lasting invitation at the Wine-Dark Harbor. Accepting opens
the Trojan camp landing as a stop for the shared Greek ship.

The camp is a named Trojan account beginning after Ajax's death. The Theban
opening keeps its own time. Every camp scene and the return to Dione name
that frame. Helen's and the Atreid household's selected accounts remain in
their own records.

## The three beats

1. **The child's shield.** Carry Ajax's shield from the camp to Tecmessa's
   household tent. Set it there for Eurysaces. Tecmessa speaks for her own
   care of the household. Eurysaces appears in her account. The carrier
   first learns through Talk that the shield belongs to Eurysaces. Teucer,
   Odysseus or Tecmessa can each say so.
2. **A rival's word.** Hear Teucer, take his written plea and carry it to
   Odysseus. The carrier must know the plea and hold its actual paper.
3. **Earth beside the ships.** Choose a public rite, a household rite, or a
   witnessed appeal. Prepare the grave with the camp's tools and lay its
   cloth there. The final burial requires both objects at the grave.

The appeal starts a vigil. Teucer and Odysseus must hear the witnessed
appeal before that route can end in burial. The burial story card closes
when Ajax has received earth. The vigil and the support given to it are
separate recorded actions.

The camp, household tent, grave and landing have direct local paths.
They belong to one camp. The ship serves the journey between its shores.

## Return to Dione

Carry the plea back to Teucer after burial. He adds the completed rite and
signs the account. A carrier who knows that account can bring its paper to
Dione. Her response names the chosen Trojan rite beside the earlier Theban
deeds. The paper stays at the courtyard after the reading.

Teucer's recorded words establish the plea and signed account. Each carrier
learns his account through conversation and carries the actual paper.

Eight original couplets cover the shield, plea, three burial forms and
three return readings. Each couplet follows its own receipt. Repeated
travel keeps the same earned poem.

## Source and adaptation

Sophocles' [_Ajax_](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/ajax.html) supplies the household, Teucer's burial claim and
Odysseus's support for a former rival. The chapter begins after Ajax's
death. Its carried objects, player actions, household route, witnessed
appeal and return to Dione are original CosyWorld adaptations. The
[epic scene guide](../../../docs/worldpacks/greek-epic-loom/tragedians.md#sophocles-ajax)
records that source boundary and the three proposed beats.

The cast IDs are `greek-ajax-tecmessa`, `greek-ajax-teucer` and
`greek-ajax-odysseus`. The chapter uses `ajax-camp-day.webp`,
`ajax-camp-night.webp`, the three matching portrait PNGs and
`ajax-shield.png`. The plea, burial cloth and tools reuse the existing
decree, funeral cloth and shovel art as separate local objects.

Greek pack revision 7 adds these people, objects and place memories.
Earlier positions, possessions, poem receipts and access remain valid.
New ship access follows the invitation. Older full-access visitors can
still use their existing paths through the camp.
