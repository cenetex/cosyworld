# The Open Door

Attica is there from the start: the Athenian Agora, Piraeus Quays, Colonus
Grove and the Eleusinian Sanctuary, with the Sacred Way, the Long Walls and
the Cithaeron road between them. Marathon is a rumour in this release (rules
94). The Open Door has no steps and no ending. Its people each wait for a
thing and each want something; the stories meet only by sharing things. The
design, its five puzzles and their orders are in
[the Open Door as interlocking puzzles](../../../docs/design/open-door-rework.md).

## Roads

Thebes reaches Attica two ways, and both need the one loaf of Dione's bread.

- **By land.** The City Gate guard stands aside while he holds Creon's writ
  or the herald's truce, and bars the way from both sides while he holds
  neither. The battlefield leads to the Cithaeron road and Eleusis.
- **By sea.** The Charybdis sails from the Wine-Dark Harbor to Piraeus with
  Myrto's Travel Provisions lying on deck. Every way home is free.

The Sacred Way (Eleusis to the Agora), the Long Walls (Agora to Piraeus) and
the Eleusis to Colonus path are plain roads with no gate.

## Who waits for what

Each resident is a locked card at its post until its thing is there. The lock
reads where things are now; its words come from the rules ("Bring Funeral
Cloth home to Eleusinian Sanctuary"). Once a traveler has met a resident, the
card stays open: the rules keep a shut resident's card untouched until it has
been met, so a resident shuts again only while nobody has met it.

| Resident | Post | Opens while | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aethra | Eleusis | the Funeral Cloth is near Eleusis | to talk with Theseus |
| Theseus | Agora | the Mothers' Petition is near the Agora | to hold the petition |
| Adrastus | Eleusis | the Herald's Truce is near Eleusis | to hold the truce |
| An Argive Mother | Eleusis | the Funeral Urn is near Eleusis | to hold the urn |
| Iphis | Eleusis | the Funeral Urn is near Eleusis | the urn laid at Piraeus Quays |
| Myrine of Argos | Eleusis | the Funeral Urn is near Eleusis | to go to Piraeus Quays |
| Thaleia | Piraeus | the Sack of Wheat lies at Piraeus | to talk with Myrine |
| The Grove Keeper | Colonus | the Funeral Oil is near Colonus | the oil laid in Colonus Grove |
| Ismene | Theban sanctuary | (open) | to talk with Theseus |
| The Bard | the harbor | (open) | to talk with Adrastus |
| The Agora Neighbor | Agora | (open) | to eat the Courtyard Bread |

Aethra, Thaleia and the Grove Keeper keep to their posts: their things can
come home with nobody there. A resident goes along with a traveler when the
trip serves its want (the petition in hand brings Theseus; the road to
Piraeus brings Myrine), and with trust 2 anywhere. A want met away from its
resident (the oil back in the grove, the urn on the quay) pays when the
resident is next spoken to or next moves.

## The things

| Thing | Home | Starts | Read by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Mothers' Petition | Eleusis | lying at Eleusis | Theseus's lock and want |
| The Herald's Truce | Dione's courtyard | in Theseus's hands, shown once he is met | the City Gate, the urn, Adrastus's lock and want |
| Funeral Cloth | Eleusis | the Theban sanctuary | Antigone's rites, the urn, Aethra's lock |
| Funeral Oil | Colonus | Dione's courtyard | Antigone's rites, the urn, the keeper's lock and want |
| The Funeral Urn | Eleusis | locked, made by Aethra | the mourners' locks and wants |
| Sack of Wheat | Piraeus | in Dione's hands | Dione's bread, Thaleia's lock |
| Courtyard Bread | Dione's courtyard | locked, made by Dione | the guard, the provisions, Patroclus, the Agora Neighbor |

Aethra makes the urn at her altar, while she stands there, from the cloth, the
oil and the truce given to her or laid at Eleusis. Nothing is used up for
good: a spent thing goes home. The rites at the Hollow send the cloth home to
Eleusis and the oil to Colonus; the urn sends them there too, and the truce
to Dione's table. Trade Theseus the petition for the truce, or walk him to
his mother with the petition in hand.

## Sources

The Eleusis scenes adapt Euripides' _Suppliant Women_: Aethra at Demeter's
altar, the mothers' plea, Adrastus, Theseus, the recovery of the dead and the
ashes. The game's readings are its own: Adrastus wanting the truce, Iphis
seeing the ashes to the quay, Ismene seeking Theseus (_Oedipus at Colonus_
ends with the daughters asking him about their father's grave) and the Bard
seeking Adrastus, who speaks over the dead. Nothing says whether Polynices
is among the Argive dead. The Grove Keeper tells Sophocles' _Oedipus at
Colonus_ as an earlier tale. Thaleia and Myrine are original.

- [Euripides, Suppliant Women](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/suppliants.html).
- [Euripides, Children of Heracles](https://classics.mit.edu/Euripides/heracleidae.html).
- [Sophocles, Oedipus at Colonus, Storr translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/31/31-h/31-h.htm).
- [Sophocles, Oedipus at Colonus, Center for Hellenic Studies](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/sophocles-oedipus-at-colonus-sb/).
