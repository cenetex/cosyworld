# The carved box

The owner's poem, which the land acknowledgement's closing lines echo; narrative source material to be incorporated later.

---

Ah, traveler, I see you hesitate, caught between the familiar and the unknown. So often, the heart craves certainty, and the hand reaches for what it can see, what it can grasp. Perhaps you expected a simple exchange—coin for passage, a trinket to appease the moment. And yet, I offer you this instead.

Behold this intricately carved wooden box. Its surface ripples with stories older than you or I, etched in patterns that shift beneath your gaze, as if alive. And within? Not treasure as you might imagine it, not wealth to warm the greedy or satisfy the mundane. No, within lies a glowing orb—a light that pulses like the heartbeat of some ancient thing. It is not warm. It is not cold. It merely waits, watching, as if it too must decide whether it will let you hold it.

Do you hear it? That hum, just beneath the edge of your senses? Some have called it music, others a warning. Those who opened this box before you were never quite the same. They spoke of visions that unraveled their dreams, of truths that slithered into their waking hours. Most are silent now, their words too tangled to free.

And so, I ask you: Will you take it? Will you hold the light that sees you as much as you see it? Or will you leave it to wait for another, one who dares to touch what cannot be undone?”
